# Football loss → country ETF · fixed protocol before retrieving outcomes

## Question and trading rule
A national team's loss may depress domestic risk appetite. Test whether weakness still exists during the next US-listed country ETF session, after the match result could be known. This tests a retail-timed daily-bar trade, not the change from the previous close, which includes movements that precede the result or an opening gap the trade cannot capture.

- Event universe: senior men's final tournaments (including group stage), FIFA World Cup, UEFA Euro, Copa América, AFC Asian Cup and CONCACAF Gold Cup in Mart Jürisoo's international results dataset. Exclude qualification/friendlies. Only losses count, including penalties. Discover losers using scores and the shootout winner where a tied match has a corresponding shootout. Event date comes from reported local match date. Keep no more than one signal per ETF per next US session.
- Universe frozen before seeing results: long-established country ETFs EWZ Brazil, EWG Germany, EWQ France, EWP Spain, EWI Italy, EWJ Japan, EWW Mexico, EWY South Korea, EWA Australia, EWK Belgium, EWN Netherlands, EWL Switzerland and EWC Canada. Proxies include USD FX effects. England excluded because EWU covers all of the UK; Argentina excluded because ARGT began partway through development and global listings complicate its domestic-market proxy. This is a chosen liquid, surviving-ETF panel, not a global census. Retain listed names even if fewer matches or missing data.
- Short one ETF without leverage notionally from first NYSE session open on a date strictly after match date to that session's close. Direction is based on the proposed mood effect. Round-trip cost 0.30% of notional (0.10% commission + 0.05% spread/slippage per side) plus one calendar day borrow at assumed annual 3%; stress 0.90% round-trip plus annual 10% borrow. These are simulated costs, actual quotes, locate and minimum commissions not modeled. Test gross diagnostically. Positive return = decline larger than cost. Chart opens are theoretical fills, not guaranteed. Positions are independent; results are per-trade and not account returns.
- Historical development 2010–2020 inclusive, locked retrospective test 2021–2025. Assign by entry date and exclude cross-boundary matches. Also report test splits 2021–2022 and 2023–2025. Planned stability checks are continental tournament versus FIFA World Cup, leave-one-ETF-out, and higher cost. The same rule is used across all. No parameter fitting.
- Matched random control: 10,000 draws using fixed seed 8771. For each trade take a same ETF, year and entry weekday non-event session without replacement within each matching group. A shared set of choices for all events on a particular entry date and across ETFs retains common-date market exposure where all affected ETFs have quotes. Compare average net return to null means using an upper-tail permutation-style p-value and report Monte Carlo distribution. Show separate SPY open-to-close-relative gross returns descriptively.
- Predeclared pass requires positive net return in the holdout, random-control p < 0.05, positive mean in each subperiod, positive stress mean, and positive results when any single ETF is omitted. Treat the p-value as descriptive when the tournament detections remain clustered in season/calendar.
- Report win rate, frequency per calendar year, worst trade, drawdown of session returns using equal shares of capital across simultaneous short signals (unlevered, flat otherwise), number of events, the actual cost scenarios and any missing coverage. Use event counts and simulation monthly ending returns for observed sixfold outcomes, without treating the count as a forecast.
- Initial data plan: Mart Jürisoo international football data and penalty shootouts, Yahoo Finance via research.events.bars. Use open-to-close prices as returned by the same function. Days absent in the ETF at the SPY-derived next session are missing, never backfilled. No live execution or existing bot changes.

## Provider feasibility amendment before examining returns

Yahoo query1/query2 returned HTTP 429 and Stooq returned an HTML gate or HTTP 404. Nasdaq's historical quote endpoint returned date coverage from late 2016 through the full 2025 holdout on a 2010–2025 query. Use Nasdaq for the entire ETF panel if Yahoo cannot serve the data and report the observed dates. Use only full calendar years available for all prices as development (2017–2020 if the first full year is 2017), with the holdout unchanged. For intraday returns use Nasdaq quoted open and close directly; a same-date adjustment factor would cancel. The baseline draws each same year and weekday control day across all ETFs with complete quotes, excludes all signal dates globally and samples without replacement across dates to retain market clustering. The provider/date probe inspected response schemas and date coverage only.

Yahoo date convention, if available: for US ETFs only, map the `research.events.bars` date back one calendar day. That helper adds 12 hours to timestamps. The US session's 13:30/14:30 UTC opening timestamp otherwise falls on the next date, which could silently introduce a pre-event entry when joining.
