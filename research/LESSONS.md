# Mistakes and lessons log (TradeLab, 2026-11-23)

Every mistake found while building and researching, how it was caught, and the fix. Keep adding to this file.

## Code mistakes
| # | Mistake | Effect | How caught | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `global acct` declared after use in `server.py` | server would not start | first launch, SyntaxError | moved `global` to top of `do_POST` |
| 2 | Theme colours are `oklch()`, chart library cannot parse them | all charts blank | headless screenshot | `css()` converts tokens to rgba through a canvas pixel |
| 3 | Chart dates followed the browser locale (Thai) | Thai dates despite "no Thai" | screenshot | `localization: {locale: "en-US"}` |
| 4 | `calm` filter used an 80th percentile of volatility over all bars | look-ahead leak: future data in train and test | review | trailing 252-bar percentile known at each bar |
| 5 | Live auto trader passed its own flat side to event rules | live could stay flat while the backtest held | review | live target = final position of the exact backtest (`simulate()["final"]`) |
| 6 | Trade tags taken from the latest entry list | a flip booked the new entry's tags on the old trade | code read | tags stored on the position at fill |
| 7 | Learning failed on `None` prices before an asset's listing | first learn crashed, then a month was marked rebalanced to cash | scratch run log | skip `None`; never rebalance before a successful learn |
| 8 | Stale pending orders could survive into a new month | wrong sizes | review | cancel pending orders at each monthly rebalance |
| 9 | `range=max` with daily interval silently returned monthly bars | history far too short | bar counts | `period1/period2` requests with adjusted closes |
| 10 | `pkill -f "python3 -u server.py"` matched its own shell | the command killed itself | exit code 144 | kill only PIDs from `pgrep -f '^python3 -u server.py'` |
| 11 | Unit tests assumed zero slippage while the default is 0.02% | 2 false failures | test run | tests set `slippage=0` explicitly |
| 12 | Yahoo per-symbol RSS returned unrelated headlines | wrong news on a symbol | smoke test | keep items that mention the ticker or company name |

## Research mistakes and traps
| # | Trap | Evidence | Rule now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Repeating backtests until something works | evolved rule +7.0%/trade in search vs +3.6% for random entry in lockbox | keep a locked period; compare with a random-entry baseline; count trials |
| 2 | Survivorship bias (today's large caps) | flatters buying after drops | re-test on stocks that shrank (`survivor_check.py`) |
| 3 | Per-trade edge mistaken for portfolio edge | dip-buy +3%/trade but portfolio did not beat SPY | always simulate slots, cash and costs |
| 4 | One bull asset driving a result | dual momentum 2017 +182% from Bitcoin | report yearly returns and asset share |
| 5 | Edges decay | selling after bad news worked 2010-2018, gone after | test on the latest years separately |
| 6 | High leverage looks good on daily closes | 20x: 6x in 18-25% of months but wiped in 54-66% | include wicks, funding and ruin rate |
| 7 | Small samples | Polymarket 490 markets: signs flip by horizon | no conclusion under a few thousand cases |
| 8 | Blocked data sources | GDELT refused every request | use the market's footprint (gap + volume) and say so |
| 9 | Secondary web figures disagree | Alpha Arena numbers differ by source | cite sources, label unverified figures |

## Conclusions so far
- Nothing tested makes 6x a month reliably; routes that can (leverage, new listings, long-shot bets) usually end at zero.
- Best tested method: monthly dual momentum over 9 ETFs plus BTC, about 10-15% a year expected, losing years of 20-30%.
- The paper auto trader runs it with 5,000 THB; forward paper results over months are the only real proof.

## World signals (2026-11-23)

| Trap | Evidence | Rule for follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| A shifted market date can silently turn an event trade into a same-day trade | Legacy Yahoo helper adds 12h. US opening timestamps roll onto the next UTC date while BTC/ETH midnight timestamps retain the original date. This changed the initial FOMC result when audited. | Join US prices on the New York session date from the raw timestamp and crypto on the UTC bar boundaries. Check a known event/session before calculating. Research scripts correct dates locally. |
| The date a value describes can precede the date it was visible | Wikimedia aggregate counts and NOAA observation archives do not have a historical first-seen record in our inputs. Delaying the assumed entry controls one timing risk and cannot prove publication. | Record first-seen UTC and revisions. Skip arrivals after the predetermined order time. Use issued weather forecast snapshots for forecast-based signals and BLS/EIA as-released releases for surprises. |
| A mechanism or a market reaction is not an executable return | Football's local-index result includes a different market window. Our US ETF intraday short averaged +0.068% gross and −0.240% net. FOMC rate futures moved inside the announcement window, and the specified SPY next-session trade averaged −0.701% net. | Separate the market's announcement window from the earliest provably executable trade. Match random controls to instrument, calendar and costs. |
| A positive headline average can disappear under basic replication | Wikipedia/BTC averaged +0.61% in the holdout with 21 fills, adjusted p=.925, −1.02% in 2023–2025, and −0.89% at higher cost. The second ETF for gas and the equity transfer asset for Fed did not rescue either rule. | Count primary and robustness variants before analysis. Check time slices, delay, cost, alternative assets, overlap and sample size. |
| Random dates can misstate an edge if the signal clusters | The cold-weather test trades mostly in winter. Football has simultaneous signals around tournament dates. | Match season/calendar, apply the same costs and holding horizon, and handle overlapping positions and clustered events in the control. |
| An available history can omit untradeable outcomes | Current liquid ETFs and established spot coins are fixed proxies. Broad meme-token results still lack historical depth, failed fills, dead pools and delisted assets. | Preserve the full point-in-time universe and execution outcomes before making a broad claim. |

Four pre-specified signal families used real events and prices. None passed its deployment gates. No sixfold month was observed under each study's price-marking convention in the 2021–2025 holdout. Details, source snapshots and rerunnable scripts are in [world_signals](world_signals/), with the Thai report in [reports](../reports/สัญญาณเทรดจากทุกสิ่งในโลก.md).
- Relaxing an option strike grid increased historical trade coverage, but produced no 6× month in the fresh Jan–Oct 2026 BTC or ETH windows. Count a single crossing of a cumulative threshold only once even if the account remains above it through a later idle month.
- Lowering a target also changes exits and cash timing. Replay the 3× spot and futures stop rules instead of relabeling 6× results. Monthly-settled options can be re-scored directly. ETH options reached 3× in 3/24 older months and 0/10 in the fresh option history.
- Expiry returns can miss a take-profit opportunity. With full-sized observed sell-aggressor prints and modeled fees, both BTC and ETH options reached 3× in the same February 2026 sell-off, though they had no 2026 expiry-only 3× month. Two correlated underlyings on the same day are one market episode, not independent replication. Prioritize frequency and distinguish modelable prints from real fill proof.
- More opportunities to place small option orders can amplify path dependence. Weekly ETH hit 3× in 7/12 separate 2025 monthly resets, and reached it in just 1/10 of the later months. A continuously funded BTC account reached a 6× monthly close twice at base costs in 2026, including once from slightly above the original starting capital, but zero times at the higher execution haircut. Always test order minima on remaining equity and realistic cost changes before treating rare wins as repeatable.
- Cash reserves can preserve trade capacity in some start periods but still be dominated by minimum order and fee interactions. The 50%-budget weekly BTC replay reached 3× twice from actual month-start balances in Jan–Oct 2026 at base costs, but only once from a balance at least as large as the original 5,000 THB. Higher friction removed that funded hit. Test full-account random direction paths with path-dependent minimums instead of relying on monthly resets alone.
- Linear-options archive quantities can already be expressed in underlying units. SOL archive trade records gave both coin amount and contract count; verify their relation before applying the multiplier, trading fees, order minimums and settlement. Weekly SOL had only 2 modeled entries in 38 later eligible weeks and no 3×/6× month. Report quote-window fill attrition when moving a rule across assets.
- An equal-sized weekly strangle needs both legs to fit the time, observed size and combined cash constraints. The fixed first-print BTC/ETH transfer yielded only two modeled base-case positions in 168 asset-weeks and no 3× or 6× months. Screen joint executability before expanding a two-leg payoff study. An incomplete November replay is a checkpoint, not a completed monthly observation.
- The VIX9D crossover rule for next-session UVXY beat matched random dates in the older sample. In the later 59-trade sample, mean net return was −1.50% versus −0.16% on random dates and the continuous account lost 63% of original capital. An early period edge can reverse even when the signal and ETF history are available throughout.
- The Kalshi archive returned decimal-dollar strings in an unsuffixed price field. Division by 100 is only appropriate for integer cents; verify representative historical and live payloads. The F1 qualifying rule showed a large monthly-reset win but turned one continuous paper wallet from 5,000 THB to about 41 THB after modeled costs.
- The OVX daily shock rule for next-session USO did not beat matched dates in either period. The later sample had only 12 events and −1.28% mean net. OVX's CSV labels its closing value OVX rather than CLOSE; check downloaded source headers rather than assuming the VIX schema transfers.
- Transferring the same 20% volatility shock rule from oil to gold produced only nine holdout GLD trades and a negative net return. The continuous gold account retained about 95% of starting cash but recorded no 3× month. Capital retention alone cannot qualify a rule for the target.
- Before ranking more signals, calculate a hindsight ceiling for the chosen execution shape. Perfect selection of positive open-to-close days at modeled costs yielded no 3× month for GLD, USO or UVXY individually over 94 months. This only eliminates that specific single-asset, same-session long shape over the observed sample.
- A high-volume spot breakout from a quiet week did not outperform same-date random liquid pairs. The rule generated 492 signal dates with no 3× month; selection on relative volume did not compensate for the modeled daily turnover costs. This is a same-universe retrospective study.
- A hindsight rank diagnostic found enough open-to-close movement across the eligible Binance universe for both capital targets, unlike the three individual ETFs. It quantifies the selection problem only. Keep the historical winning days out of any independent verification period and check observable pre-entry signals and real transaction evidence.
- Reconcile a near-target month with the individual drivers. One ALPACA trade after the delisting announcement drove the best month (2.92×); removing just this trade turns the 2024–25 baseline account from about 7,565 THB into 2,574 THB. Matched randomized comparisons do not fix concentration in a single event.
- Binance spot aggTrades CSV encodes buyer-maker as capitalized True/False and recent timestamps as microseconds. Parse both explicitly. Replaying observed buyer- and seller-aggressor quantities for ALPACA supported the order size proxy at the time boundaries, but an earlier exit and delayed entry reduced the already subtarget multiple. Treat observed print capacity separately from guaranteed execution.
- The later spot flush slice also depended on one trade: omitting its best result reduced the ending baseline wallet from 7,608 to 4,543 THB. Quantify single-trade concentration separately in each date slice. Calendar-block resampling is descriptive and cannot establish independence across months.
- Transaction-count spikes with little contemporaneous price change were frequent but had negative next-day spot returns after costs. An unconstrained fractional replay can keep trading past venue order minima; report this as an idealized test and reject the rule before execution assumptions become relevant.
- A November checkpoint selected 60 pairs by prior-month liquidity before fetching new bars. All 1,320 expected bars were present. Two frozen spot rules stayed in cash and the third lost on all three modeled selections. Label the restricted universe and partial month so absence of signals is not mistaken for a full monthly test.
- Widening the November spot checkpoint from 60 to 177 pairs revealed additional signals, but every active rule still lost after modeled costs. Universe expansion on overlapping dates and pairs fixes coverage and does not add independent time blocks.
- Earlier intraday entry changes the available observations. The four-hour taker-buy rule selected four fresh November trades and still lost after costs. An entry at the very next bar open assumes negligible decision latency; validate it with a delayed trade print if a larger test shows a viable edge.
- The frozen four-hour spot imbalance rule produced just one losing October signal in 60 preselected liquid pairs. Test entry frequency across disjoint full months before extrapolating from a partial calendar window.
- Expanding the frozen opening-flow rule to July–September added seven trades without a profitable month. A new signal window should be screened for both plausible event frequency and after-cost payoff before finer execution modeling.
- The specified hourly sell-off and recovery pattern occurred once in 145 days of the fixed liquid-pair rotation. Reporting months with zero signals avoids treating flat months as evidence of clean execution or positive edge.
- A 4% weekend BTC rise occurred before just four tradable BITX Mondays in the 34-month sample. The three favorable early examples did not replicate in the later slice. Use actual leveraged ETF prices and separate the already cleared opening gap from returns after entry.
- An ED4 surprise stored as −0.0499999999999971 is exactly −5 basis points at source precision. Apply an explicit precision convention before comparing thresholds. The BITX transfer had five trades across the historical and updated Fed snapshots and no 3× month.
- BITX open/close price moves capped even a perfect cash-or-long timing strategy at 2.79× after baseline costs over 40 historical months. Compute an exact two-state hindsight bound to account for reduced turnover when holding across adjacent winning legs. This does not cover intraday quotes or trading other instruments.
- Rotating among four ETFs expanded the hindsight ceiling versus picking a single ETF, but even perfect daily decisions reached 3× only twice in 40 months at baseline costs. The fixed momentum rule underperformed random fund choices in the later slice. Treat cross-asset opportunities separately from executable selection skill.
- A zero-trade 2026 Bybit delist run reflected title schema coverage, not absence of spot delist events. Full Bybit article pages contained exact symbols even when the catalog only listed token counts. Join the publication timestamp to those article pairs and persist coverage before claiming a fresh holdout.
- Full Bybit article text recovered 44 dated USDT pairs across 11 notices in 2026. No candidate passed the frozen rebound rule. Article-text coverage still excludes image-only notices, USDC-only removals and publication timestamps after effective removal.
- The Bybit delisting day +3 rebound transfer kept entries and exits before the article-confirmed end dates. It filled the available bar proxy for 44 pairs, but net losses exceeded the same-pair earlier control. Separate one-off rebounds from repeatable event timing.
- The hourly event follow-up materially changed signal timing, but seven of 44 pairs lacked valid signal bars and none of the 37 observable pairs met the locked 10% decline. A no-trade result has no paired return estimate.
- The eligible F1 contract menus and settlement timing limited attainable upside even under perfect outcome knowledge. The monthly-reset oracle produced 3× in 3/22 months and 6× in none under the archived quantity proxy. Treat menu coverage and realized predictive skill as separate bottlenecks.
- The NYC temperature one-trade oracle changes sharply with observed size: 2025 sixfold months fell from five to two at base costs and from four to one at stress. The oracle identifies capacity limits, not selection skill or an execution guarantee.
- Smaller order fractions and earlier recycling did not rescue the New York temperature model. The fixed 25% wallet allocation had 0/32 wins in 2025 and depleted the account to ten cents. Separate treasury rules from forecast discrimination before spending effort on position sizing.
- Decomposing the daily temperature rule exposed both market-data and rank failures: of seven quoted low-price winners in its 2025 query universe, four had no preceding-hour trades, one was below the model trigger, and two lost to competing choices. Future-hour prints cannot replace the predecision liquidity signal.
- On the 304 paired 2026 Central Park days, the frozen 80% GFS predictive interval covered only about 73%. A shorter lead reduced MAE slightly without fixing interval calibration. Check forecast quality on fresh outcomes before downloading thousands of market quotes.
- A fresh, evenly sampled 2026 weather-price transfer captured all 75 targeted quote pairs on 20 dates. The unchanged daily model lost all 15 trades under both costs. Sparse market dates are enough to reject this probe but cannot estimate full-month hit frequency.
- Fresh ETH option prints permitted one November order from a new wallet, but the inherited balance could not meet that trade's minimum. Time holdout checks should carry earlier account equity alongside independent fresh-start diagnostics.
- Weekly BTC/ETH rotation by the largest absolute prior return did not preserve the common wallet. Report target months from a depleted balance separately from months that start with adequate original funding, and model cross-asset rules in one account rather than adding standalone returns.
- The weekly BTC/ETH one-position oracle enumerated both directions, venue assets and skip decisions. Even knowing the future, with a fresh wallet and high modeled friction only four of 22 complete months hit 6× and eleven hit 3×. The option tape leaves opportunity, but not across all months under these constraints.
- Cross-asset agreement can appear to improve an option wallet simply by skipping weeks. Match the number of skipped weeks before crediting the informational filter. The 2026 baseline produced one funded 3× month, which disappeared at stress costs and did not replicate on the 2025-start account.
- A CPI calendar gate did not rescue weekly BTC options. Only six modeled purchases occurred in the continuous account and none achieved either monthly target. A historical release date does not establish the contemporaneously knowable calendar version, especially across postponements.
- The prior-Friday VIX direction generated one funded 3× month in the baseline 2026 BTC option replay. This did not survive higher costs or an earlier account start. Independent feature data do not by themselves make a retrospective joint replay a live holdout.
- Deduplicate funded BTC option target months across strategy variants using underlying timestamp and venue trade ID. Two of three baseline rule hits reused the exact same April sell print; the third used a January sale. A repeat at the same trade is not a second validation.
- A size-qualified exit can be highly transient. The January funded target relied on two sufficient sell prints within 2.907 seconds, unlike April's longer sequence. Reassess the actual sell window before treating isolated threshold crossings as executable.
- A weekend-only BTC direction variant preserved one funded January 3× hit under higher costs by changing the preceding trade, but reused the same January option as the VIX result and the wallet then lost nearly all capital. Distinguish surviving one stress episode from surviving a continuous stress path.
- Acquiring 46 previously unused 2024 weekly BTC option dates did not replicate the weekend rule's January 2026 hit. A genuine calendar transfer adds evidence while preserving the fixed rule and continuous cash path. Do not assume a fresh January wallet existed after earlier simulated losses.
- Applying the frozen VIX and seven-day BTC direction variants to 2024 produced no funded target month. Only the seven-day rule tripled from depleted capital. Count variants on one evaluation tape separately from distinct market episodes.
- Audit of the 24-hour pre-removal Bybit rebound (2026-11-23): stored hourly bars matched a refetch 919/919 and NEON's +238% lead-in was genuine price action, not a unit error. 43/44 pairs last traded in the 07:00 UTC bar, confirming the 08:00 UTC stop; notices preceded entry by 140–168 h.
- Do not drop zero-volume klines at fetch time. All 137 "missing" hours in the pre-removal input were zero-volume bars, which produced both the 19/44 signal coverage and the 368/1,000 unverified random paths. A random control that discards incomplete paths is biased; with few events, enumerate all paths and report drop, carry and loss treatments (here 15–25% of 20 paths matched or beat the two-trade rule).
- Run the perfect-hindsight ceiling before building a rule. Buying the best pair 24 h before Bybit removal and selling at the best bar high still reached 3x in only 1/10 months (one AO event), and only 5/10 months had any notice. The constraint was event count, not selection skill.
- Check the ceiling of the exact locked structure before locking it. The weekly BTC oracle of 11/22 months at 3x belonged to a one-direction trade; the two-leg DVOL strangle (25%+25%, one leg expires worthless) had a hindsight ceiling of only 2/22 months, and the unchanged DVOL-low rule lost on average (0.77x per week vs 0.86x for other weeks) on the 2025-26 tape. Inherit headroom claims only for the same payoff structure.
- A retrospective consistency check was run before the first forward snapshot and changed nothing in the protocol. Fetch paged APIs (Deribit delivery prices are capped at 100 per page) so old weeks are never silently skipped.
