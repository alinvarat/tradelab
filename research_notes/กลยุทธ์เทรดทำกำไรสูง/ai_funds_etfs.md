# AI-Driven Funds, ETFs and Quant Firms with Public Track Records, 2015-2026 (research as of 24 Nov 2026)

Scope note for the report writer: everything below is *live-money* evidence unless explicitly labelled backtest. ETF returns are NAV total returns, i.e. **net of the expense ratio but before spreads, commissions, taxes and any platform fee**; hedge-fund returns are net of manager fees where the source says so. Where a number comes from a secondary aggregator rather than the filing, it is flagged. The USD 147 account constraint is treated as binding: cash account, no margin, no shorting, no futures.

## 1. AI-themed ETFs, AI stock-picking ETFs and robo-advisors: returns vs SPY/QQQ, fees, drawdowns, since inception — is AIEQ's AI adding value?

### Takeaway
No. The only long-running fund where an AI actually picks the stocks, AIEQ, has returned **9.4-9.6%/yr since Oct 2017 against 14.6-14.7% for the S&P 500**, a 4.9-5.1 pp/yr shortfall that is ~85% explained by the model's decisions and only ~15% by its 0.75% fee. Thematic "AI" ETFs are a coin flip determined by index definition, not by intelligence: over their own lifetimes BOTZ, ROBT and ARKQ lagged the S&P 500, while AIQ and CHAT (both launched into/after the 2023 generative-AI melt-up) beat it. Robo-advisors are not AI alpha at all — they are static ETF allocation, and their 8-year "outperformance" is explained by a US large-cap/muni tilt.

### Cited Findings

**AIEQ, the cleanest live test of "AI picks stocks"**
- AIEQ launched 17 Oct 2017, runs EquBot's model on IBM Watson, screens news/social media/analyst reports/filings; expense ratio **0.75%**, net assets **$107.2m**, 161 holdings as of 17 Nov 2026. Since 29 Jan 2024 the fund is *passive*, tracking the AI Powered Equity Index — the AI still selects, but the fund no longer trades discretionarily — [Amplify fund page](https://amplifyetfs.com/AIEQ); [summary prospectus](https://amplifyetfs.com/wp-content/uploads/files/Amplify_AIEQ_Summary_Prospectus.pdf).
- Official average annual returns to 31 Dec 2025: **1 yr 13.80%, 5 yr 5.75%, since inception 9.40%**; S&P 500 **17.88%, 14.42%, 14.62%**; after-tax-on-distributions since inception 8.73%, after-tax-plus-sale 7.27% — [prospectus, p.6](https://amplifyetfs.com/wp-content/uploads/files/Amplify_AIEQ_Prospectus.pdf).
- Fiscal year to 30 Sep 2025: **9.60%/yr since inception vs 14.73%** S&P 500; 5 yr **8.71% vs 16.47%**; annual cost **$82 per $10,000 (0.75%)**; **portfolio turnover 804%** — [annual shareholder report](https://amplifyetfs.com/wp-content/uploads/files/AIEQ_Annual_Report.pdf).
- $10,000 at inception grew to **$20,732** vs **$29,829** in the S&P 500 to Sep 2025; the 5.13 pp/yr gap is 14.6% fee, 85.4% model/implementation — [FinanceGrub, 17 Jul 2026](https://financegrub.com/can-ai-predict-the-stock-market-eight-years-of-a-real-ai-fund).
- Schwab report card to 30 Sep 2026: 5-yr **4.4%/yr**, since inception **9.6%**; $10,000 → **$22,846** vs **$34,307** for S&P 500 TR; 1-yr 9.7% vs 15.7% — [Schwab PDF](https://www.schwab.wallst.com/cgi-bin/upload.dll/file.pdf?z0e8f7c0az2b61079ee04c42eabcf59d82c6b42de5).
- Month-end 31 Oct 2026: since inception **9.37%/yr**, 5-yr **3.13%/yr**, 3-yr 19.68%, YTD +7.44% — [Amplify](https://amplifyetfs.com/AIEQ).
- 2022 calendar year **−31.57%**; worst quarter −21.05% (Q1 2020); 2018 **−7.28%** vs S&P −4.75%, 2019 **+31.14%** vs +33.07% — [summary prospectus](https://amplifyetfs.com/wp-content/uploads/files/Amplify_AIEQ_Summary_Prospectus.pdf); [Buczynski et al. 2021, §Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8019690/).
- Cumulative since launch to 6 Nov 2026: **+35.2% vs +54.6% for SPY**; 1-yr +5.8% vs +10.9%; typical spread **0.29%**, full cost of ownership **1.08%/yr** at $10,000 (vs 0.75% headline fee) — [ETFIQ](https://etfiq.com/funds/aieq).
- **97.1% of AIEQ's weight is stocks the S&P 500 already holds; active share 72.1%; top ten 43.8%**; largest positions American Express 9.0%, Caterpillar 4.6%, Microsoft 4.0% (6 Nov 2026) — [ETFIQ](https://etfiq.com/funds/aieq). This is a mid-cap/blend active bet expressed through large caps, not a diversifying "AI" exposure.
- Peer-reviewed characterisation: AIEQ is the "closest we have come across to an AI fund success story", and only because it merely underperformed — [Buczynski, Cuzzolin & Sahakian, *Int J Data Sci Anal* 11:221-242, Apr 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-021-00245-5).

**Academic tests specific to AI ETFs**
- 25 active + 22 passive AI ETFs: **passive beat active** over full trading history without materially higher volatility; both groups trade at a **persistent premium to NAV**; **the majority generate no significant alpha**; market factors explain performance — [MDPI *JRFM* 19(4):267, Apr 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/19/4/267).
- May 2019-Sep 2024, daily returns net of stated expense ratios: AI thematic and AI-powered ETFs **underperform both XLK and the S&P 500**; Fama-French 5-factor + Newey-West shows **no abnormal return for AI ETFs**, while an equal-weight Magnificent-7 portfolio does show significant positive alpha — [Antar, *Computational Economics*, 21 Feb 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10614-026-11320-z).
- AIEQ herds like institutions: **29% average holdings overlap** with institutional investors, and an "AI gap" appears in bear markets due to strategic inflexibility (sample Oct 2024-Jun 2025, single fund — weak external validity) — [Deng, *SAGE Open*, Dec 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440251398597).
- 2022-2024: AI-driven funds had worse Sharpe but better Treynor/Jensen in the 2022 downtrend; human funds won in the 2023-24 uptrend — [Anuar et al., *Future Business Journal* 11:95, 2 May 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43093-025-00540-8).

### Inferences
- AIEQ is not a fee problem. At 0.75% it would need ~**0.75 pp/yr of gross alpha just to cover the fee**, and ~**5.1 pp/yr** to have matched the S&P 500 since inception; it has delivered roughly −4.4 pp/yr net of the benchmark. The 804% turnover is the mechanism: an edge of a few tenths of a percent per forecast is consumed by spread/impact when the book is replaced eight times a year.
- AIEQ is also not really an "AI diversifier". With 97% of weight in S&P 500 names and 72% active share, its tracking error comes from sector/stock tilts (industrials overweight, financials underweight, AmEx 9%), which is exactly what a discretionary mid-cap blend manager does.
- Thematic AI ETF dispersion (BOTZ 8.5%/yr vs AIQ 17.8%/yr, same 0.68% fee, same issuer) is index-construction luck. Any claim that "AI ETFs beat the market" is a statement about launch date and index rules, not about intelligence.
- Robo-advisors should be scored as cheap discretionary portfolio management. Their fees (0.25%) are close to irrelevant versus a 0.03% index fund; the allocation policy is the product.

### Gaps
- No public source gives AIEQ's **maximum drawdown since inception** on a daily NAV basis. Only the 2022 calendar (−31.57%), worst quarter (−21.05%, Q1 2020) and worst 3 months (−18.08%) are documented.
- Amplify does not disclose AIEQ's holdings-level attribution or the model's signal decay; the "85% model vs 15% fee" split is arithmetic on the return gap, not an attribution study.
- No peer-reviewed study isolates AIEQ's alpha with a proper factor model over the full 2017-2026 window (Deng 2025 covers only 9 months).

## 2. Quant/ML hedge funds with public letters or databases: returns, drawdowns, capacity, fees, and how much is ML vs other edges

### Takeaway
The genuine outliers are **capacity-constrained, short-horizon, execution-heavy shops** (Medallion, XTX, Squarepoint), and their returns are not transferable to outside capital: Medallion has been employee-only since 2005, XTX trades its own book, and Squarepoint's Form ADV reserves its shortest-term signals for its higher-fee funds. The *investable* quant products — Renaissance's public funds, Two Sigma, Man AHL — look like ordinary active managers: roughly market-like returns with lower beta, long stretches of underperformance, and 20-35% drawdowns.

### Cited Findings

**Renaissance Technologies**
- Medallion 1988-2018: **66.1%/yr gross, 39.1%/yr net** (average); **63.3%/yr compounded gross**, so $100 gross → **$398.7m**; **no negative year before fees in 31 years, exactly one after fees (1989)**; 2000 +98.48% net, 2008 +82.38% net; fees rose to **5% + 44%** in 2002 — [Cornell Capital Group, Feb 2020 / JPM reprint](https://www.cornell-capital.com/uploads/papers/medallion-fund.pdf); [Visual Capitalist, 19 Jun 2024](https://www.visualcapitalist.com/growth-of-100-invested-in-jim-simons-medallion-fund).
- 2020: Medallion **+76% net** while RIEF **−19%**, RIDA **−31%**, RIDGE **−31%** — [Business Times/Bloomberg, 8 Feb 2021](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/banking-finance/renaissance-clients-pull-out-after-firms-rotten-run-of-results); HSBC marks RIEF at −22.62% and RIDA −33.58% through 25 Dec 2020 — [Institutional Investor, 13 Jan 2021](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswms7wco7as686o8ikg/portfolio/renaissances-medallion-fund-surged-76-in-2020-but-funds-open-to-outsiders-tanked).
- RIEF (launched Jul 2005) annualised **8.05%** vs S&P **9.6%** as of Jan 2021; longest drawdown May 2007-Apr 2009 **−35.73%**; 2008 −16%, 2009 −6.17% — [Institutional Investor, Jan 2021](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswms7wco7as686o8ikg/portfolio/renaissances-medallion-fund-surged-76-in-2020-but-funds-open-to-outsiders-tanked). By Feb 2021 RIEF's since-inception annualised was **9.1% vs 9.7%** for the index, and it needed +37% to get back to even after January's −9.5% — [Bloomberg via WealthAdvisor, 12 Feb 2021](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/jim-simons-makes-billions-while-renaissance-investors-fume-losses).
- The 2020 failure was a **beta-model control failure**: the system added exposure in Jan 2020, reversed in Q1, then "overcompensated" and was over-hedged during the Apr-Jun rebound; the firm said the model "anticipates that in track records as long as ours, some risk-return ratios every bit as bad as the ones we are now seeing are not shocking" — [Business Times/Bloomberg, Feb 2021](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/banking-finance/renaissance-clients-pull-out-after-firms-rotten-run-of-results).
- External AUM collapse: **$65.1bn → $23.2bn** (2020-2024); RIEF $35.8bn → $19.6bn; RIDA+RIDGE $29.3bn → $3.6bn; 2021 RIEF +14.6%, RIDA +20.1%; 2024 RIEF +22.7%, RIDA +15.6%, Medallion +30% — [FT/Alphaville, 20 Sep 2024](https://www.ft.com/content/0d93cc76-4b1f-4c83-afdf-c940f180db08); [Hedgeweek, 14 Jan 2025](https://hedgeweek.com/news/renaissance-tech-and-two-sigma-lead-2024-quant-gains).
- **Data caveat**: Renaissance publishes nothing. Medallion's numbers come from the appendix of Zuckerman's 2019 book, reproduced by Cornell; no audited statement has ever been public, and the "net" column is a mechanical fee calculation, not reported NAVs — [James Folk, 3 Sep 2026](https://jamesfolk.me/writing/how-renaissance-technologies-makes-money). A 2024 reconstruction using fund size and trading profits puts the 1988-2018 compounded return at **31.8% before fees**, not 63%, and argues the true figure is "probably under 35%" — [Guo & Liu, arXiv 2405.10917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10917). Treat the 66%/39% as a widely repeated reconstruction, not an audited fact.

**Two Sigma**
- 2024: flagship Spectrum **+10.9%**, Absolute Return Enhanced **+14.3%**; 2023: Compass **−6%**, Compass Enhanced **−16%**, Spectrum +8.6%, ARE +12% — [Hedgeweek, Jan 2025](https://hedgeweek.com/news/renaissance-tech-and-two-sigma-lead-2024-quant-gains); [Bloomberg, 29 Jan 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-01-29/two-sigma-s-macro-compass-enhanced-fund-sank-16-last-year).
- Two Sigma Diversified (inception 2016): **+61.1% cumulative to 2024** (~6.2%/yr over 8 years), Q3 2025 **−0.7%**, YTD 2025 **+5.7%**; losses came from Alpha Capture and Fundamental models, gains from Event and Technical models — [Hedge Fund Alpha, 24 Nov 2025](https://hedgefundalpha.com/investor-letters/two-sigma-diversified-fund-q3-2025).
- China onshore Dingliang CSI 500 index-enhanced strategy **+52.5% in 9M 2025, +23 pp over benchmark; 20.8%/yr since 2023 inception**; onshore AUM passed RMB 10bn — [Bloomberg, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-12/two-sigma-joins-china-hedge-fund-elite-after-strategy-soars-52).

**Man AHL (the most transparent long-run systematic record)**
- AHL Diversified (Ireland, inception 26 Mar 1996), USD: **9.60%/yr since inception, vol 16.08%, Sharpe 0.43, max drawdown −29.98% (13 months, 9-month recovery)**; calendar: 2022 **+12.46%**, 2023 **−3.46%**, 2024 **−0.24%**, 2025 **+5.69%**, 2026 YTD to Jun **+11.68%** — [fact sheet, 31 Dec 2025](https://swissfunddata.ch/sfdpub/docs/fsm-2320_00_01-20251231-en.pdf); [30 Jun 2026](https://swissfunddata.ch/sfdpub/docs/fsm-2320_00_01-20260630-en.pdf).
- AHL Diversified Futures (Bermuda, 12 May 1998): since inception **6.44%/yr, vol 14.53%, Sharpe 0.27, max DD −31.57%**; 12 months to Oct 2026 +21.79%, but Oct 2024-Oct 2025 only +3.40% and Oct 2023-Oct 2024 **−10.59%** — [fact sheet, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.man.com/document?doc-type=TAP&instrument=rs19895&locale=en).
- 12 months to Mar 2025: AHL Diversified **−18.4%**, 3-yr −3.8%/yr, 5-yr +1.0%/yr, since inception +9.4%/yr — [Man Group trading statement, 17 Apr 2025](https://www.man.com/documents/download/22b67-3e7d-45546-a70cb/Press_Release_Man_Group_plc_-_Trading_statement_for_the_quarter_ended_31_March_2025_English_17-04-2025.pdf?type=view). Man Group AUM **$213.9bn** at 30 Sep 2025.

**XTX Markets (market making; the strongest per-head economics in the dataset)**
- 2025: combined net revenue **£3.93bn** (2024 £2.74bn), net profit **£1.71bn** (2024 £1.28bn); the IP/services entity employed **127 people** at year-end and earned £1.69bn after tax; two FCA trading entities had revenue £849m and £62m — [Companies House filing via FinanceMagnates, 3 Apr 2026](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/brokers/xtx-markets-revenue-rises-43-to-393-billion-in-2025/); [XTX Markets Technologies FY2025 accounts](https://s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/document-api-images-live.ch.gov.uk/docs/ETY_A9pp1lt22PjAnOLkkkJhxZk3d-ezhV9o-Pn0VRY/application-pdf).
- XTX discloses no fund return, no Sharpe, no investor capital — it is a proprietary market maker. Any "AI fund" comparison to XTX is a category error.

**Squarepoint (capacity rationing is explicit)**
- Founded 16 Dec 2014 as a Barclays systematic-trading MBO; **regulatory AUM $227bn at 31 Dec 2025** (gross, includes borrowing, up from $34.6bn in an earlier ADV); ~2,542 staff; **4,299 13F positions**, millions of trades/day — [Form ADV via Edgepedia](https://www.edgechat.ai/squarepoint-capital); [teardown, 3 Jan 2026](https://www.navnoorbawaresearch.com/p/squarepoint-capital-teardown-statistical).
- July 2026 Form ADV: the firm "reserves shorter-term signals" whose capacity "has been reserved for the **Higher Fee Funds**"; on the analyst's reading, outside holders of Focus and Atlas (and perhaps Core) get the slower, higher-capacity version of the strategy — [analysis of the ADV, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.navnoorbawaresearch.com/p/squarepoint-capital-keeps-shorter).
- No public fund return series. A 13F long-book "1Y portfolio return +18.2%" is a holdings artefact, not a fund return — [HedgeThink, Oct 2026](https://www.hedgethink.com/top-hedge-funds/squarepoint-ops).

**Industry aggregate for "AI" hedge funds**
- Eurekahedge AI Hedge Fund Index: **9.8%/yr Dec 2009-Jul 2024 vs 13.7% for the S&P 500**; January 2022 **−3.38% vs −1.11%** — Eurekahedge data as reported by IG on 21 Nov 2024, tabulated at [AI Trading Book, 27 Aug 2026](https://aitradingbook.com/does-ai-trading-work.html) (secondary: the IG article itself was not retrieved). Index construction involves self-selection and survivorship that flatter the index, so the true gap is if anything wider.

**Bias flags for this whole section**
- Hedge-fund databases (HFR, Eurekahedge, PivotalPath, BarclayHedge) are **voluntary, self-reported and survivorship-biased**: dead funds stop reporting, and backfilled early returns are common. No audited public NAV series exists for any of the fund-level numbers above; Man AHL fact sheets and SEC ETF filings are the only official documents here.
- Medallion is the extreme case: a legendary, non-audited, insider-only track record that cannot be allocated to. Any statistic derived from it (including "39%/yr") is a reconstruction.
- The external Renaissance funds are the honest counter-sample: same firm, same software, same senior management, and roughly market returns. Asness's summary, quoted by FT: "When they do something at institutional size for outside investors they look human" — [FT, 20 Sep 2024](https://www.ft.com/content/0d93cc76-4b1f-4c83-afdf-c940f180db08).
- Two Sigma's AUM ($60bn) and Squarepoint's ($227bn gross) are **regulatory AUM**, not investable net capital; they overstate capacity available to a new investor.

### Inferences
- The pattern across the investable managers: **net returns roughly equal to or below the S&P 500, with lower beta and lower vol**. Man AHL's 9.6%/yr since 1996 with Sharpe 0.43 and −30% drawdowns is a fair description of what a diversifying systematic product actually delivers. RIEF's 8-9%/yr vs 9.6-9.7% is the same story.
- Where ML plausibly *is* the edge — Medallion, XTX, Squarepoint — the edge is bundled with (a) short horizon, (b) enormous data/execution infrastructure, (c) capacity rationing, and (d) in Medallion's and XTX's case, no outside capital at all. The "ML" label is not the differentiator; the plumbing is.
- Two Sigma's case is the cleanest evidence that the binding risk in professional ML is **governance, not prediction**: $165m of client harm came from write access to a parameter database, not from a bad forecast. The same failure mode exists in any retail model with an editable config.
- Capacity disclosure (Squarepoint) is the single most useful institutional tell: if a manager openly tiers its signals by fee, the outside investor is, by construction, not getting the best model.

### Gaps
- No public, audited, continuous return series exists for Medallion, XTX, Squarepoint, Citadel, D.E. Shaw or Two Sigma's private funds. All numbers are press reports or letters.
- No source quantifies what fraction of any of these firms' P&L is ML versus execution, data cleaning, financing, or market-making spread capture. The firms do not disclose it, and the "AI fund" label is applied by journalists and index providers.
- Squarepoint, XTX and Medallion fee schedules for external capital (where any exists) are not public in comparable form.

## 3. Crowd-sourced ML (Numerai) and retail AI signal platforms: out-of-sample performance, survivorship, payout structure

### Takeaway
Numerai is the only crowd-sourced ML fund with a multi-year live record and SEC filings, and its record is real but thin: **+25.45% net in 2024, −17% in 2023, ~+8% in 2025**, on a market-neutral book that grew from ~$60m to ~$550m AUM. The *contributor* economics are far worse than the fund's: net payout per round fell from **179bp (2020) to 8.7bp (2026)**, the payout factor from **1.00 to ~0.11**, and roughly **half of all staked model-rounds now burn**. Retail signal platforms (Danelfin, Kavout) publish provider-run backtests with no peer-reviewed validation; the one genuinely independent 6-month test is n=40 and directional only.

### Cited Findings

**Numerai fund**
- Founded Oct 2015; fund (Numerai One) inception **1 Sep 2019**; fees **1% management + 20% incentive** (Founding Class); **2024 net return +25.45%, Sharpe 2.75, one down month**; 2023 **−17%**; ~+6% net in 2025 to August — [Numerai blog, 26 Aug 2025](https://blog.numer.ai/jpmorgan-secures-500m-capacity); [Hedgeweek, 28 Aug 2025](https://www.hedgeweek.com/quant-hedge-fund-numerai-secures-500m-jpmorgan-allocation).
- JPMorgan Asset Management committed **up to $500m of capacity** (Aug 2025); AUM grew ~$60m → **$550m** over three years, plus $100m in one month; Series C $30m at **$500m valuation** (Nov 2025) — [Numerai blog, 20 Nov 2025](https://blog.numer.ai/numerai-raises-30m-series-c-at-500m-valuation).
- 2025 to October: "estimated **8%**" vs **~3%** for the PivotalPath Equity Quant Index — [PivotalPath, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.pivotalpath.com/insights/paul-tudor-jones-backed-numerai-nabs-500-million-valuation).
- Form ADV: **$1,903,690,264 RAUM at 31 Dec 2025**; management fee 1.0% or 2.0% depending on fund — [Numerai GP ADV Part 2A, 31 Mar 2026](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=1039534).
- 13F: position count **111 → 746** while average position size stayed flat ($1.32m → $1.34m); Dec 2025 13F long US equity ~$830m across 608 holdings. Regulatory AUM is gross and not comparable to net AUM — [nmrdash, 12 Jul 2026](https://nmrdash.com/articles/numerai-hedge-fund-performance).
- There is **no continuous public NAV or audited fund-return series**; performance exists only as isolated company announcements and 13F/ADV filings — [nmrdash](https://nmrdash.com/articles/numerai-hedge-fund-performance).

**Numerai tournament / contributor payout structure**
- Scoring: CORR (correlation to target) + **MMC** (meta-model contribution); since 2024 the primary equity tournament pays **MMC-only**; payouts capped at **±5% of stake per round**; **1 NMR minimum payout** (stakes under ~100 NMR often fail to process) — [AlphaNova, 28 May 2026](https://www.alphanova.tech/blog/is-numerai-worth-it).
- Net payout per round as share of everything staked: **179bp (2020), 161bp (2021), 62bp (2022), 12.5bp (2023), 13.8bp (2024), 8.5bp (2025), 8.7bp (2026 YTD)**; average payout factor **1.00 → ~0.10-0.112** as average NMR at risk per round rose from ~115k to ~693k — [nmrdash, 12 Jul 2026](https://nmrdash.com/articles/numerai-alpha-decay).
- Burn rate: **26-29% of staked model-rounds burned in 2020-2022**, spiked to **60% mid-2023**, averaged **46% in 2025**, **~50% in 2026** (peak 59% early 2026); share of models with positive MMC fell **52% (2021) → 42% (2026)** — [nmrdash](https://nmrdash.com/articles/numerai-alpha-decay).
- Cumulative paid to data scientists **>$43m**, >$1m in Jan 2025 alone — [AlphaNova](https://www.alphanova.tech/blog/is-numerai-worth-it). Lifetime tournament flows: **3.12m NMR earned, 1.28m burned, 1.84m net distributed**; 2026 net Classic payouts ~**1,000 NMR/round, ~0.15% of the staked pool per round** (not an annual return) — [nmrdash, 28 Apr 2026](https://nmrdash.com/articles/state-of-numerai-2026).
- Survivorship: a **63% one-week cleanup** of stakers, rebuilt to 82% of the late-2024 count; **6,467 active models** and 830,184 NMR locked (Apr 2026). Fund RAUM $1.045bn vs **NMR market cap $62.7m** — the token is 1/17th the size of the fund it feeds — [nmrdash](https://nmrdash.com/articles/state-of-numerai-2026).

**Retail AI signal platforms**
- Danelfin's own audit: US stocks AI Score 10 **+21.05% annualised 3-month alpha vs S&P 500**, Score 1 **−33.28%**, Jan 2017-Apr 2025; Europe +35.40%/−11.75% vs STOXX 600 (2018-Aug 2025); US ETFs +12.80%/+0.26% (2017-Oct 2024) — [Danelfin help centre, 28 Aug 2026](https://help.danelfin.com/en/articles/are-ai-scores-reliable-how-successful-were-ai-scores-predictions-in-the-past). These are **provider-run backtests**, and the numbers were revised between vintages (the Nov 2024 page said +14.69%/−37.38% for the US) — [older page](https://support.danelfin.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404382344465-Are-AI-Scores-reliable-How-successful-were-AI-Scores-predictions-in-the-past).
- Danelfin's methodology page discloses: tree ensemble on trailing 252 days, 1-10 score = probability of beating the S&P over 90 days; live tracking only began **1 Jul 2025**; everything before is a backtest over three non-overlapping windows; no peer-reviewed validation exists as of May 2026; pricing €29/€79/€179 per month — [Acutic field guide, 2 Jun 2026](https://acutic.io/blog/ai-stock-scores-field-guide).
- Danelfin's price-band backtest (Jun 2016-May 2026) uses **300 currently-active US stocks** and explicitly warns this "introduces mild survivorship bias"; 68% of realised 6-month returns fell inside the band; 6-month windows overlap ~23 of 26 weeks, so the effective sample is far smaller than 300×519 — [Danelfin accuracy page](http://danelfin.com/ai-stock-forecast-accuracy).
- The only independent forward test found: 40 stocks scored 9-10, Jan-Jun 2026, **+11.3% vs +6.8% S&P**, with 40 random S&P names as control; author explicitly calls it directional, single-window, n=40 — [InsigTrade, 14 Jul 2026](https://insigtrade.com/blog/danelfin-review-does-the-ai-stock-rating-actually-work).
- A 90-day, 400-signal, four-platform test (Trade Ideas, Tickeron, Danelfin, TrendSpider) found **none beat a 55% win rate as a group**; best cell was Danelfin score 8+ on a 10-day hold at **61%**; the claimed inter-platform accuracy gap collapsed from 25 points to 13 once the universe and holding period were standardised — [InsigTrade, 7 Jul 2026](https://insigtrade.com/blog/ai-trading-signals-accuracy-compared-across-platforms).
- Kavout (Kai Score 1-9), Seeking Alpha Quant, Simply Wall St and TipRanks are covered in the same field guide: none has peer-reviewed validation; most disclose factor families but not weights — [Acutic](https://acutic.io/blog/ai-stock-scores-field-guide).

**The crowd-sourcing cautionary tale**
- Quantopian raised **$48.8m**, had **210,000 community members by Aug 2018**, and Point72 committed **up to $250m** to algorithms it surfaced. Its market-neutral fund stopped producing returns, returned investor money in **Feb 2020**, and the platform shut **14 Nov 2020** — [Business of Business, 9 Nov 2020](https://www.businessofbusiness.com/articles/how-quantopian-died-shut-down-quant-investment-robinhood); [Neudata, 11 Nov 2020](https://www.neudata.co/alternative-data-news/quantopian-shuts-its-doors-co-founders-join-robinhood).
- Quantopian's own 2016 study "All That Glitters is Not Gold" found users' **backtest performance metrics had "little value in predicting out of sample performance"** — [QuantRocket, 4 Nov 2020](https://www.quantrocket.com/blog/quantopian-shutting-down).

### Inferences
- Numerai's fund and its contributors are two different businesses. The fund can be genuinely good (2024 Sharpe 2.75) while the median contributor loses: with ~50% of staked rounds burning and a payout factor near 0.11, the tournament is a **winner-take-most filter**, not a yield product. Contributor return is also denominated in NMR, whose market cap ($62.7m) is small enough that a $500m JPM allocation cannot be hedged inside the token.
- The payout-factor collapse is a direct measurement of **alpha decay under crowding**: the same platform, same target, same scoring, paying 95% less per unit of stake in 2026 than in 2020. That is the best available empirical proxy for "how fast does a public ML signal decay".
- Danelfin's score separation (10 vs 1) is large and monotonic, which is what a working cross-sectional signal looks like. But the evidence is provider-run, revised across vintages, survivorship-flagged by the provider itself, and untested after costs/turnover. The burden of proof is on the vendor and it has not been met independently.
- The 90-day multi-platform test is the most useful retail datapoint: after standardising universe and hold period, the platforms cluster near a coin flip. Most of the apparent edge in vendor marketing is definitional.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed out-of-sample study of Numerai's meta-model returns or of Danelfin/Kavout scores exists. The only forward tests found are small and short.
- Numerai does not publish a monthly NAV series, Sharpe by year, or drawdown; the 2023 −17% and 2024 +25.45% are company/press statements.
- No source quantifies the **after-cost** return of following Danelfin's top decile (turnover, spread, slippage) or the tax drag.
- The minimum capital to stake meaningfully on Numerai is undisclosed in USD terms; the 100 NMR payout threshold and the token's thin market cap make a $147 position uneconomic.

## 4. Which AI methods are disclosed and what evidence shows they work outside a backtest

### Takeaway
Disclosed methods cluster into four families: **cross-sectional factor mining with trees/NNs** (Gu-Kelly-Xiu lineage, Two Sigma, Numerai, Squarepoint), **NLP/text extraction** (AIEQ/EquBot, AI analysts), **execution/market making** (XTX, Squarepoint, Medallion), and **trend/risk premia** (Man AHL's Diversified programme; the ML share of that programme is not disclosed). The out-of-sample evidence is consistent: ML forecasts have genuine but tiny predictive R² (~0.3-0.4% monthly), the long-short Sharpe of 1.3-2.5 is gross and concentrated in microcaps, and **cost-aware portfolio construction is what separates a paper Sharpe from a live one**.

### Cited Findings
- Gu, Kelly & Xiu, *RFS* 33(5):2223-2273, 2020 (30,000 stocks, 94 characteristics, 1957-2016): neural network **value-weighted long-short decile Sharpe 1.35, equal-weighted 2.45**; OLS benchmark 0.61/0.83; timing the S&P 500 with NN forecasts gives **Sharpe 0.77 vs 0.51 buy-and-hold**; monthly out-of-sample R² ~**0.40%**; trees and NNs win because of nonlinear interactions; all methods agree the dominant signals are **momentum, liquidity and volatility** — [RFS abstract](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/33/5/2223/5758276).
- The microcap dependence is explicit in the follow-up: excluding microcaps, the plain conditional-autoencoder model's out-of-sample R² collapses from **0.27% to 0.03%**, while a transaction-cost-aware version holds **0.27%** and lifts the long-short Sharpe from 1.442 to **1.512** (full sample) — [Jo, Kim & Shin, Jun 2025](https://kasba.or.kr/download?name=Stock%20Return%20Predictability%20and%20Machine%20Learning%20Incorporating%20Transaction%20Costs.pdf&path=%2Fuploads%2Fpapers%2FNODE12408985.pdf).
- The decisive critique: "numerous studies use machine learning return forecasts to generate portfolios, their agnosticism toward trading costs leads to excessive reliance on fleeting small-scale characteristics, resulting in **poor net returns**"; the fix is optimizing portfolio weights *with* costs, i.e. the "implementable efficient frontier" — [Jensen, Kelly, Malamud & Pedersen, Feb 2023](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=32368).
- Direct neural-network weight optimization: gross Sharpe **4.06** (1987-2021), but the cost-aware version's average monthly transaction cost is **0.525%** (52.5% combined long+short turnover per month) and its after-cost Sharpe is **3.344** — a number that only makes sense with institutional-scale trading and is not a retail attainable result — [Yuan & Zhang, 29 Jul 2025](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=196560).
- NLP: an AI analyst trained on corporate disclosures, industry trends and macro data **beats most human analysts** at predicting returns, but humans retain an edge on intangible assets and distressed firms; the combination beats either alone, mainly by cutting extreme errors — [Cao, Jiang, Wang & Yang, *JFE* 160, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2024.103910), summarised at [FinanceGrub](https://financegrub.com/can-ai-predict-the-stock-market-eight-years-of-a-real-ai-fund).
- Disclosure in live products is minimal. AIEQ/EquBot: "up to 10 years of historical data", news, social media, analyst reports, filings, monthly rebalance, proprietary weighting — [Amplify](https://amplifyetfs.com/AIEQ). No feature importance, no signal decay, no attribution.
- Two Sigma's own letter names its model families (Alpha Capture, Fundamental, Event, Technical) and their monthly P&L contributions; that is unusually granular for a live fund — [Hedge Fund Alpha, Nov 2025](https://hedgefundalpha.com/investor-letters/two-sigma-diversified-fund-q3-2025).
- Squarepoint describes "a rigorous scientific approach", strategies "originate from backtests on historical data", fully automated execution of millions of trades/day — [Squarepoint](https://www.squarepoint-capital.com/); its ADV rations shorter-term signals by fee tier.
- The peer-reviewed review of 27 ML equity experiments (1994-2019) found the literature's headline accuracy metrics (MAPE, RMSE, hit rate) are not comparable across studies, most experiments are long-only and cost-blind, and there is **"no conclusive evidence of any ML-driven investment funds delivering spectacular returns at scale"** — [Buczynski et al., 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-021-00245-5).
- Chen & Ren, *Finance Research Letters*, 2022 (CRSP, 2009-2019, 2,133 new funds, prospectuses hand-labelled): AI-powered mutual funds **do not outperform the market**, but **do outperform human-managed peers**, attributed to **lower transaction costs, better stock selection and fewer behavioural biases** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612321005547). This is the one peer-reviewed result that gives AI a genuine, if relative, edge.

### Inferences
- Sort the evidence by where the AI sits. **Forecasting** has a small, real, decaying edge (R² ~0.3-0.4%/month, mostly in illiquid names). **Portfolio construction and execution** is where the money is made or lost: cost-aware optimization, turnover control and execution quality dominate the forecast itself. **NLP** has the clearest incremental value, but as an analyst assistant, not an allocator.
- The 1.35-2.45 Sharpes in the academic literature are gross, long-short, and heavily microcap-weighted. A long-only, unlevered, $147 account cannot harvest the short leg, cannot short at all, and would be trading the most crowded, most liquid end of the signal where the premium is smallest.
- Nothing in the disclosures supports the marketing claim that a fund "uses AI" as a differentiator. Every live manager that discloses enough to check (AIEQ, Man AHL, Two Sigma, Renaissance external funds) produces market-like or below-market net returns.

### Gaps
- No live fund discloses its **out-of-sample signal decay curve** or feature importance. AIEQ's 804% turnover is the closest public proxy for "the model is chasing short-lived signals".
- No study measures net-of-cost ML performance for a **long-only US large-cap** universe at retail size; the literature is long-short and institutional.
- Reinforcement learning in live trading is essentially undocumented publicly; the only supervisory evidence is second-hand (AFM's survey of Dutch prop firms, which notes RL "could potentially lead trading algorithms to learn undesirable behaviour") — [IOSCO CR/01/2025, p.62](https://api.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD788.pdf); [AFM, Oct 2023](https://www.afm.nl/~/profmedia/files/rapporten/2023/report-machine-learning-trading-algorithms.pdf).

## 5. Regulatory disclosures warning about AI trading, and failures/blow-ups involving AI/ML

### Takeaway
Regulators have converged on a consistent warning list — **explainability, over-reliance, data quality, concentration/herding, procyclicality, model risk, and governance of who can change the model** — but have produced almost no AI-specific binding rules. The SEC's 2023 predictive-analytics rule was **withdrawn in June 2025**, leaving enforcement (AI-washing, model-governance failures) as the operative constraint. The documented failures are overwhelmingly **implementation, cost and control failures, not "the AI was wrong"**: Renaissance's beta model in 2020, Two Sigma's writable parameters, Aidyia/Sentient's commercial failure, Quantopian's crowd failure.

### Cited Findings

**Regulator positions**
- FINRA, *Artificial Intelligence in the Securities Industry*, 10 Jun 2020: AI is already deployed in customer communications, investment processes and operations; the report is explicitly **not an endorsement**; firms must address **model risk management, data governance, customer privacy, supervisory controls**, plus cybersecurity, outsourcing and books-and-records. It notes vendor concentration could "concentrate risks related to errors or malfunctioning of AI systems" — [FINRA report](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/fintech/report/artificial-intelligence-in-the-securities-industry); [full PDF](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2020-06/ai-report-061020.pdf).
- SEC, 18 Mar 2024 — first "AI washing" settlements: **Delphia (USA) Inc.** claimed it used client data to predict which companies would grow; it had **not used client data and had not built the algorithm**; **$225,000** penalty. **Global Predictions Inc.** called itself the "first regulated AI financial advisor" and could not substantiate it; **$175,000** — [SEC press release 2024-36](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-36).
- SEC, 16 Jan 2025 — Two Sigma: **$90m** penalty, **$165m** voluntarily repaid, for failing to address known model-access vulnerabilities from 2019 to 2023 and for failing to supervise the modeler. This is the template for "AI model risk" enforcement: the failure is controls, not alpha — [SEC 2025-15](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15).
- SEC, 26 Jul 2023 proposal on **Conflicts of Interest Associated with the Use of Predictive Data Analytics** would have required broker-dealers/advisers to identify and **eliminate or neutralise** conflicts from AI/ML/deep learning/neural nets/LLMs and even lookup tables and correlation matrices; **formally withdrawn 12 Jun 2025**, effective 17 Jun 2025; the SEC says any future action needs a new proposal — [SEC rule page](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/06/s7-12-23); [Federal Register 90 FR 25531](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/17/2025-11110/withdrawal-of-proposed-regulatory-actions); [Paul Hastings, 18 Jun 2025](https://www.paulhastings.com/thought-leadership/sec-withdraws-14-pending-rule-proposals).
- ESMA, *Artificial intelligence in EU securities markets*, 1 Feb 2023: risks are **explainability, concentration/interconnectedness/systemic risk, algorithmic bias, operational risk, data quality and model risk**; these are "amplified when using AI, as AI systems typically operate at greater scale, complexity, and automation than traditional statistical tools" — [ESMA50-164-6247](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/ESMA50-164-6247-AI_in_securities_markets.pdf).
- ESMA **Public Statement on AI in retail investment services**, 30 May 2024: warns of **over-reliance, lack of transparency/explainability, security/data privacy, robustness and hallucination**; "hallucinated information can lead to misleading advice"; firms must test and monitor AI and keep human judgment — [ESMA35-335435667-5924](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ESMA35-335435667-5924__Public_Statement_on_AI_and_investment_services.pdf).
- ESMA **Supervisory Briefing on Algorithmic Trading in the EU**, 26 Feb 2026: AI used in algo trading must be folded into MiFID II RTS 6 governance, annual self-assessment and validation; firms need the **"unconditional capacity to monitor, suspend, or terminate algorithmic trading"** without the vendor's consent; AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689) applies where the system meets the "AI system" definition, and AI in trading is currently **not** a high-risk use case but that scope is reviewed annually — [ESMA74-1505669079-10311](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-02/ESMA74-1505669079-10311_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Algorithmic_Trading_in_the_EU.pdf); [Macfarlanes, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.macfarlanes.com/insights/102mpep/algorithmic-trading-and-artificial-intelligence-esma-supervisory-briefing/).
- IOSCO, *AI in Capital Markets: Use Cases, Risks and Challenges*, CR/01/2025, Mar 2025, reports the Dutch AFM's finding that a significant share of prop traders' algorithms used ML "at their core", often predicting price moves from **order-book data with at least 100 parameters**, predominantly supervised; firms cared more about **performance than explainability**, and RL could learn undesirable behaviour. AFM's five risks: explainability, manipulation (of and by models), procyclicality, concentration, knowledge gaps — [IOSCO PDF, p.62](https://api.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD788.pdf).
- FSB, *Financial Stability Implications of AI*, Nov 2024 (via IMF/CEF presentation): warns that **market-manipulation risk is amplified by increased correlations from widespread use of common AI models**, producing synchronised trading — [IMF CEF slides, 20 May 2025](https://www.cef.imf.org/content/dam/CEF-IMF/CEF-IMF-NewSite/3-News/1-CEFNewsandEvents/3-SeminarsWebinarsHigh-levelEvents/2025-05-20%20-%20Webinar%20on%20Advances%20in%20Artificial%20Intelligence%20Implications%20for%20Capital%20Market%20Activities%20Presentation.pdf).
- FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09 (2024) on generative AI: no new requirements, but firms must maintain reasonably designed supervisory systems covering AI, including model risk management and data reliability — summarised at [LegalClarity, 9 Jul 2026](https://legalclarity.org/sec-predictive-data-analytics-rule-proposal-and-withdrawal).

**Failures and blow-ups**
- **Aidyia** (Hong Kong, Ben Goertzel): launched a fully autonomous AI fund in Jan 2016 with "no human intervention"; **liquidated in under a year** on disappointing performance — [Business Times, 19 Jun 2023](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/wealth/ais-difficulties-learning-investing); [WIRED, 25 Jan 2016](https://www.wired.com/2016/01/the-rise-of-the-artificially-intelligent-hedge-fund/).
- **Sentient Technologies / Sentient Investment Management**: raised **$143m** in VC, ran an evolutionary-computation fund from 2016; **+4% in 2017, ~0% in 2018, liquidated in 2018** after less than two years — [Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/wealth/ais-difficulties-learning-investing); [Buczynski et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-021-00245-5).
- **Rogers AI Global Macro ETF**: launched Jun 2018, closed Jul 2019; opened at **$24.97 and closed at $24.97** (plus ~$0.60 dividends) — i.e. zero price return in its life — [Buczynski et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-021-00245-5).
- **EquBot AI Powered International Equity ETF**: liquidated after four years; the US version (AIEQ) survives but trails the S&P 500 — [Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/wealth/ais-difficulties-learning-investing).
- **Renaissance 2020**: the external funds' risk models added exposure into the Covid crash and then over-hedged the rebound; RIEF −19%, RIDA −31%, RIDGE −31% while SPY returned about **+18.3%** for the year, triggering **>$5bn of redemptions in three months** — [Business Times/Bloomberg, Feb 2021](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/banking-finance/renaissance-clients-pull-out-after-firms-rotten-run-of-results); SPY 2020 from [MEDGE](https://medgecapital.com/etf/qqq).
- **Two Sigma 2021-2023**: $165m client losses and >$400m unintended gains from tampered live models; $90m SEC penalty — [SEC 2025-15](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15).
- **Quantopian 2020**: crowd-sourced fund returned capital after its market-neutral strategy failed for ~2 years, then shut the platform; 210,000 users, $48.8m raised, Point72's $250m commitment — [Business of Business](https://www.businessofbusiness.com/articles/how-quantopian-died-shut-down-quant-investment-robinhood); [QuantRocket](https://www.quantrocket.com/blog/quantopian-shutting-down).
- **Numerai 2023**: −17% for the flagship, its worst year, followed by +25.45% in 2024 — [Hedgeweek, 28 Aug 2025](https://www.hedgeweek.com/quant-hedge-fund-numerai-secures-500m-jpmorgan-allocation).
- Note on scope: the classic algo failures often cited as "AI" (Knight Capital 2012, the 2010 Flash Crash, Volmageddon 2018) are **not** ML/AI failures; they are deployment/control and product-structure failures. No source found documents a market-wide crash caused by an ML trading model. Say so rather than blur the categories.

### Inferences
- The regulatory message is stable across FINRA (2020), ESMA (2023, 2024, 2026) and IOSCO (2025): **the model is not the supervised object; the firm's governance of the model is**. The two SEC actions that actually landed (AI-washing, Two Sigma) were about misrepresentation and access control, not forecast quality.
- The withdrawal of the SEC's predictive-analytics rule means US retail AI products face **no AI-specific rule**; the effective constraints are the Marketing Rule, Reg BI, fiduciary duty and antifraud. A retail buyer should therefore expect marketing claims to be policed only after the fact.
- The failure pattern is consistent and instructive: **AI funds die from (a) costs/turnover, (b) regime change the model was not trained on, (c) control failures, (d) capacity**. Not one public failure is attributable to the model being "not smart enough" in a static sense.
- ESMA's 2026 briefing is the most operational public checklist for anyone running a model: testing, change control, cumulative-impact assessment of small edits, an unconditional kill switch, and staff who understand what the AI does. That checklist applies equally to a $147 spreadsheet.

### Gaps
- No regulator has published a quantified estimate of AI-driven trading's share of volume or of AI-attributable losses. ESMA's 2025 work on AI in EU investment funds says funds that promote their use of AI "still represent a minor share of the industry" — [IMF CEF slides, 20 May 2025](https://www.cef.imf.org/content/dam/CEF-IMF/CEF-IMF-NewSite/3-News/1-CEFNewsandEvents/3-SeminarsWebinarsHigh-levelEvents/2025-05-20%20-%20Webinar%20on%20Advances%20in%20Artificial%20Intelligence%20Implications%20for%20Capital%20Market%20Activities%20Presentation.pdf).
- No enforcement action or postmortem was found that attributes a *specific* loss to an ML forecast error (as opposed to controls, costs or regime change). That absence is itself a finding.
- The SEC's withdrawn PDA proposal's economic analysis (costs/benefits) is not summarised here; it would be the best public estimate of conflicted-AI harm.

## 6. What a USD 147 account can learn or copy, and what requires institutional data/infrastructure

### Takeaway
At $147 the *ideas* are copyable but the *products* mostly are not: fees and subscriptions are 1-3 orders of magnitude too large relative to the account, shorting/leverage (where most of the documented alpha lives) is unavailable, and the data that makes ML work (survivorship-free, point-in-time, tick-level) is institutional. What is genuinely copyable is the **discipline**: benchmark-relative since-inception measurement, turnover/cost budgeting, cost-aware portfolio construction, and a written model-governance rule.

### Cited Findings
- Minimums and fees that bind at $147: Wealthfront requires a **$500 minimum deposit** at 0.25%/yr — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/money/research/largest-robo-advisors/). Betterment charges 0.25%/yr, **or a flat $5/month for accounts under $24,000** — same source. A $147 Betterment account paying $5/mo would pay **$60/yr = 41% of principal**.
- Danelfin pricing **€29/€79/€179 per month** — [Acutic](https://acutic.io/blog/ai-stock-scores-field-guide). At €29/mo (~$34) that is **~$405/yr, or 276% of a $147 account per year**.
- AIEQ's real cost of ownership at $10,000 is **1.08%/yr** (0.75% fee + ~0.29% spread amortised), and its typical spread is **0.29%**; a round trip costs roughly **0.58%** — [ETFIQ](https://etfiq.com/funds/aieq). On $147 a single round trip is ~$0.85, i.e. **~77% of one year's 0.75% fee**.
- Share prices are not the constraint: AIEQ ~$46.91 (6 Nov 2026), BOTZ ~$29.49, QQQ ~$645 — fractional shares make all of them accessible. [ETFIQ](https://etfiq.com/funds/aieq); [Global X](https://www.globalxetfs.com/funds/botz); [Schwab QQQ](https://www.schwab.wallst.com/Prospect/Research/etfs/performance.asp?symbol=QQQ).
- The academic ML long-short results require **shorting the bottom decile** and, in the stronger specifications, microcaps: value-weighted Sharpe 1.35 drops from equal-weighted 2.45, and excluding microcaps the plain model's R² falls from 0.27% to 0.03% — [Gu-Kelly-Xiu](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/33/5/2223/5758276); [Jo-Kim-Shin](https://kasba.or.kr/download?name=Stock%20Return%20Predictability%20and%20Machine%20Learning%20Incorporating%20Transaction%20Costs.pdf&path=%2Fuploads%2Fpapers%2FNODE12408985.pdf).
- Numerai's tournament is open to anyone, but payouts are capped at **±5%/round**, have a **1 NMR minimum** (stakes under ~100 NMR often fail), and ~**50% of staked rounds burn** in 2026 — [AlphaNova](https://www.alphanova.tech/blog/is-numerai-worth-it); [nmrdash](https://nmrdash.com/articles/numerai-alpha-decay). The NMR token's market cap was **$62.7m** in Apr 2026, so a small stake is a leveraged bet on token liquidity as well as on skill.
- Free/open infrastructure inherited from the failed crowd platforms: **Zipline, Alphalens, Pyfolio, Trading Calendars** were open-sourced by Quantopian and remain on GitHub; the community platform shut 14 Nov 2020 — [Quantopian retrospective](https://community.quantopian.com/c/quantopian-newsletter-archives/what-happened-to-quantopian); [rip.so](https://rip.so/quantopian.html).
- The public-data substitutes that are actually free: SEC EDGAR filings and Form N-PORT/13F, FRED, Treasury/ETF issuer fact sheets and prospectuses, daily OHLCV from vendors. What is missing versus CRSP/Compustat is **survivorship-bias-free history and point-in-time fundamentals**; Danelfin's own backtest concedes that using "300 currently-active US stocks… introduces mild survivorship bias" — [Danelfin](http://danelfin.com/ai-stock-forecast-accuracy).
- Institutional-only inputs visible in the sources: Squarepoint's tick-level data and millions of trades/day; XTX's market-making infrastructure (£3.9bn revenue, 127 staff in the IP entity); Medallion's short-horizon capacity (~$10-12bn internal, closed since 2005); Two Sigma's model-governance and compliance apparatus (which still failed) — [Squarepoint teardown](https://www.navnoorbawaresearch.com/p/squarepoint-capital-teardown-statistical); [FinanceMagnates/XTX](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/brokers/xtx-markets-revenue-rises-43-to-393-billion-in-2025/); [Cornell](https://www.cornell-capital.com/uploads/papers/medallion-fund.pdf).

### Inferences (arithmetic for the $147 account)
- **Fee budget.** A 0.75% ETF costs **$1.10/yr** on $147. A single 0.29% spread on a round trip costs **~$0.85**. A $5/mo platform fee costs **$60/yr**, 41% of principal. Therefore: any product with a monthly subscription or a >0.10% fee is structurally uncompetitive at this size, regardless of its alpha.
- **What is copyable at $147:** (1) one or two broad, cheap ETFs as the core, with the AI experiment as a satellite of ≤10-20% (~$15-30); (2) the measurement discipline — record every buy/sell with date, price, spread and the benchmark's same-period return, and judge only on since-inception after-cost numbers; (3) the "cost-aware" rule from the literature — require the expected edge per trade to exceed **round-trip spread + commission** before trading, which at $147 means **very few trades per year**; (4) a written governance rule for any model (who can change parameters, what triggers a kill switch), which is exactly what ESMA's 2026 briefing and the Two Sigma case demand.
- **What is not copyable:** market-neutral long-short (no shorting), microcap alpha (no borrow/locate, and the effect is a liquidity premium), intraday/execution edges (no co-location or direct market access), capacity-constrained manager returns (Medallion/Squarepoint/XTX are closed or tiered), and statistically valid ML research (survivorship-free data, point-in-time fundamentals, and enough compute to run 94-characteristic panels).
- **Realistic expectation.** If the professional, well-resourced, live-money record is ~9.4-9.6%/yr vs 14.6% for the S&P (AIEQ) and 9.8% vs 13.7% for the AI hedge-fund index, the base rate for a $147 AI strategy beating a cheap S&P 500 ETF after costs is **low single digits**. The rational use of AI at this size is research and screening, not allocation.

### Gaps
- No source prices CRSP/Compustat, point-in-time fundamentals or tick data for a retail buyer; the "institutional data" cost is therefore asserted qualitatively, not quantified.
- No study measures a **$147-scale** implementation of any published ML strategy (commissions, minimum increments, fractional shares, tax). All cost estimates in the literature are institutional.
- No public evidence on whether fractional-share ETF trading at a retail broker achieves the quoted spread; the 0.29% AIEQ spread is a quoted median, not an executed cost.
- The "what to copy" list above is my synthesis; no single source endorses a specific allocation for a $147 account.
