# Market Wizards and Famous Traders: What Is Documented, What Is Codable, and What a $147 Account Can Actually Use

Research notes compiled 2026-11-24. All returns are as reported by the cited source unless marked audited. "Audited" here means an independent CPA/accounting review of financial statements or broker statements; most hedge-fund figures below are **administrator NAVs and investor letters, not public audits**.

## 1. Documented returns, drawdowns, period, leverage, instruments and method — and which records are auditable

### Takeaway
The strongest documented records are fund-level, long-horizon, and mostly **not** publicly audited: Medallion (66.1% gross / 39.1% net, 1988–2018, one ~30% drawdown in 1989), Thorp's Princeton/Newport (19.1% gross / 15.1% net, 1969–1988, 227 of 230 months positive), Tudor (~19.5% long-run, +125.9% in 1987, then low-single-digit years and redemptions by 2016), Duquesne (30.4%, 1981–2010, no full-year loss), and Caxton (21%, 1983–2011, one losing year, −2.5% in 1994). The retail-famous records (Larry Williams, Kullamaggie, Ross Cameron, Minervini) are contest or self-reported records over short windows, and several are contradicted by later losses or regulator findings.

### Cited findings
- Medallion averaged 66.1% gross and 39.1% net per year from 1988 to 2018, per Gregory Zuckerman's reconstruction; these are "reported historic estimates for a private fund", not a current audited series — [Quantt](https://quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history); [Zuckerman/Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/557104/the-man-who-solved-the-market-by-gregory-zuckerman/).
- Medallion lost 4.1% in 1989 and suffered a peak-to-trough drawdown near 30%; Simons stopped the system for about six months and rebuilt it — [Quantt](https://quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history).
- A year-by-year table reconstructed from Zuckerman shows 1988 +16.3% gross / +9.04% net, 1989 +1.0% gross / −3.2% net, 1990 +77.8% gross / +58.24% net; the 1989/1990 figures conflict slightly with the −4.1% and ~55% net figures elsewhere, probably due to fee assumptions — [MoneyScience table](https://moneyscience.org/articles/medallion-fund.php); [Quantt](https://quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history).
- A 2024 paper argues 66.1%/39.1% should not be called CAGR because capital caps and annual distributions break the compounding path — [Liu, arXiv 2405.10917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10917).
- Medallion stopped taking new outside money in 1993 and bought out the last outside investors by 2005; fees were 5% management and 44% of profits in the 2000s — [Quantt](https://quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history).
- Renaissance told a US Senate subcommittee that Medallion's predictions were only slightly better than coin-flip and the model relied on many recommendations and the law of large numbers — [Renaissance Senate statement, 22 Jul 2014](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/imo/media/doc/STMT%20-%20Renaissance%20(July%2022%202014)1.pdf).
- The 2014 Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations report documents Renaissance's use of leveraged basket options with banks; the exact leverage ratio is not public — [Senate PSI report](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/imo/media/doc/REPORT-Abuse%20of%20Structured%20Financial%20Products%20%28Basket%20Options%29%20%287-22-14,%20updated%209-30-14%29.pdf).
- Thorp's Princeton/Newport Partners ran 1969–1988 and compounded at 19.1% gross / 15.1% net, with 227 of 230 months positive — [Chat With Traders](https://chatwithtraders.com/video/edward-thorp-math-genius-who-beat-the-dealer-and-the-market); [The 7 Circles](https://the7circles.uk/ed-thorp-innovator).
- A 1988 LA Times report, while PNP was under investigation, said the fund had compounded 19.6% since founding and "never had a money-losing quarter" — [LA Times, 5 Aug 1988](https://latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-08-05-fi-8302-story.html).
- Tudor BVI Global had long-run annual returns near 19.5%, but by 2014 had not hit that level for 11 years — [NYT DealBook, 5 Mar 2014](https://archive.nytimes.com/dealbook.nytimes.com/2014/03/05/after-a-dazzling-early-career-a-star-trader-settles-down).
- Tudor returned +3.5% in 2014, +1.4% in 2015, and −2.8% in Q1 2016, followed by heavy redemptions — [Barron's, 2016](http://barrons.com/articles/paul-tudor-jones-hedge-fund-reportedly-hit-with-big-withdrawals-1460667625); [Bloomberg, 20 Apr 2016](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-04-20/hedge-funds-endure-biggest-outflows-since-2009-in-first-quarter).
- Tudor's 1987 return was +125.9% after fees, on a roughly 200% gross return, from shorting before Black Monday — [Altrady](https://www-staging.altrady.com/blog/crypto-trading-strategies/historical-trading-performance); [Kagels Trading](https://kagels-trading.com/blog/paul-tudor-jones).
- Jones used the 200-day moving average as a regime filter and has described limiting risk to ~1% per trade with 5:1 reward/risk — [Shell Capital](https://shell-capital.com/paul-tudor-jones-200-day-moving-average-rule); [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3738365/as-the-market-crashed-in-1987-paul-tudor-jones-made-100-million-in-a-single-day-the-most-important-rule-of-trading-is-to-play-great-defense-not-great-offense).
- Duquesne averaged ~30.4% a year from 1981 to 2010 with no losing year, then returned outside money — [Reuters, 18 Oct 2010](https://reuters.com/article/duquesne-investing-druckenmiller-idUSN1819602020100818); [Reuters, 2010](https://reuters.com/article/business/duquesnes-druckenmiller-retiring-after-30-years-idUSN18196020).
- Duquesne was down ~5% in 2010, the final partial year, which qualifies the "never a losing year" claim — [Globe and Mail](https://theglobeandmail.com/globe-investor/druckenmiller-closing-hedge-fund-firm/article1377462).
- In 1987 Druckenmiller reversed from 130% long to short after realizing he was wrong, and finished the month with a net gain — [Business Insider, 2016](https://businessinsider.com/stanley-druckenmiller-response-to-black-monday-2016-11).
- Caxton returned 21% a year from 1983 to 2011 and had only one losing year, 1994, when the main fund fell 2.5% — [NYT DealBook, 13 Sep 2011](https://dealbook.nytimes.com/2011/09/13/bruce-kovner-founder-of-caxton-to-step-down/); [FT, 2011](https://www.ft.com/content/c4d8f626-de2f-11e0-9fb7-00144feabdc0).
- Larry Williams turned $10,000 into $1,147,607 in the 1987 Robbins World Cup, +11,376%, still the contest record — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_R._Williams); [Trading Tournaments](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/larry-williams-trading-career).
- The contest account peaked above $2.1M mid-year and drew back to $1,147,607 — a ~$1.0–1.4M intra-year drawdown — [Trading Tournaments](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/larry-williams-trading-career).
- An NFA panel found that in Q1 1987 Williams' composite client performance lost $6,122,281 while his personal contest account gained $902,599; Williams was fined $13,000 and Robbins $15,000 after appeal — [RealTraders archive reproducing the NFA findings, 7 Apr 1999](https://purebytes.com/archives/realtraders/1999/msg02468.html).
- Williams' Larry Williams Financial Strategy Fund lost more than 50% of client equity in about a year and closed in Oct 1989; the World Cup Championship Fund also lost over half and closed in May 1990 — [RealTraders archive](https://purebytes.com/archives/realtraders/1999/msg02468.html); [Chicago Tribune, 12 Mar 1989](https://chicagotribune.com/1989/03/12/futures-group-elects-target-of-its-own-probe).
- The Turtles earned an average annual compound return of ~80% over four years, and ~$175M in five years, according to Dennis/Faith — [Original Turtle Rules](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/01/turtle-rules.pdf); [Investopedia](https://investopedia.com/articles/trading/08/turtle-trading.asp).
- Qullamaggie reports 268% CAGR for 2013–2019, 0.3–0.5% risk per trade (rarely >1%), 5–25% positions, a 25% win rate in 2019, a 50% drawdown in 2014, and attempts to cap drawdowns at 15–20% — [Qullamaggie FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq).
- Qullamaggie's own tweet says he started 2021 with $3.7M and finished with $36M, +870% — [X post](https://x.com/qullamaggie/status/1344626907573342208); his FAQ separately lists $82M by 1 Mar 2021, an inconsistency in self-reported figures — [Qullamaggie FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq).
- A secondary account says he lost ~$60M from the Nov 2021 peak to the mid-2022 low, about half of it giving back open profits — [Complete Traders Edge](https://completetradersedge.com/kristjan-kullamagi-qullamaggie).
- A Reddit thread citing Swedish public tax records says he lost ~$25M in 2022; this is not an audited trading statement — [r/qullamaggie](https://reddit.com/r/qullamaggie/comments/1ajhm96/how_did_qullamaggie_lose_25_million_in_2022/).
- Ross Cameron's company says he turned $583.15 into more than $10M by end-2022, with broker statements reviewed by SingerLewak; other company pages claim $18M — [Warrior Trading verified earnings](https://warriortrading.com/ross-camerons-verified-day-trading-earnings/); [Warrior Trading support](https://support.warriortrading.com/support/solutions/articles/19000096593-does-ross-really-trade-with-real-money-is-he-actually-profitable-).
- The FTC sued Warrior Trading and Cameron in April 2022, alleging deceptive earnings claims; the company paid $3M and was barred from baseless earnings claims, and the FTC said "the vast majority of customer accounts actually lost money" — [FTC press release, 19 Apr 2022](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises).
- Minervini won the 2021 US Investing Championship $1M+ stock division with +334.8% among 338 international traders; the contest verifies entrants' brokerage statements — [PR Newswire, 2022](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/stock-trader-wins-us-investing-championship-a-second-time--breaks-record-301466652.html); [Wall Street Trader](https://wallstreettrader.substack.com/p/how-mark-minervini-won-us-investing).
- Minervini's 1997 win was +155% using only stocks — [Minervini Private Access](http://minerviniprivateaccess.com/about-mark).

### Inferences
- The "great track record" sample is dominated by fund managers with institutional infrastructure, not by retail-accessible methods. Medallion's return is a **capacity-constrained, employee-only, short-horizon stat-arb** result; it is not evidence that a public signal set earns 39% net.
- Reported long-run returns cluster at 19–30% net for the macro/equity legends, with much lower recent returns. The 30%+ figures come from periods of lower assets, less competition, and higher leverage.
- The largest documented drawdowns among the "winners" are 30% (Medallion 1989), 50% (Kullamaggie 2014), and >50% (Larry Williams' client funds). The "no losing year" claims are calendar-year claims, not drawdown claims.
- Contest records are **selection machines**: a leveraged account in a favorable regime can produce a 10,000% return and still be followed by a >50% fund loss within two years (Williams). A one-year contest is not a track record.
- The book/interview sample ("Market Wizards", "Unknown Market Wizards") is survivorship-biased by construction: it interviews traders who survived long enough to be interviewed and agreed to talk. Traders who used the same method and blew up are absent, so the base rate of success cannot be inferred from the books.

### Gaps
- No public, audited, investor-level return series exists for Medallion, Tudor, Duquesne, Caxton, or the Turtles. Exact leverage ratios, Sharpe ratios, and maximum drawdowns are not disclosed.
- Thorp's PNP maximum drawdown and leverage are not publicly documented in the sources found; only the 3-losing-months/230 figure.
- Tudor's and Caxton's worst peak-to-trough drawdowns (as opposed to worst calendar year) could not be sourced.
- Druckenmiller's Duquesne drawdown history is not public beyond the 2010 partial-year −5% and an early $20–25M loss.

## 2. Which methods are rule-based enough to code, and which depend on discretion, information or scale unavailable to retail

### Takeaway
Codable with public data: Turtle/Donchian breakout + ATR sizing, moving-average trend filters, volatility targeting, simple momentum/breakout stock selection, and defined-risk option structures. Not codable at retail scale: Medallion-style short-horizon stat arb, global macro discretion, event-driven/merger arb, hard-to-borrow short selling, and anything requiring prime brokerage, locates, or hundreds of PhDs.

### Cited findings
- The Turtle system is fully specified: N = 20-day EMA of true range; 1 unit = 1% of equity / N; entry on 20-day (System 1) or 55-day (System 2) Donchian breakout; add 0.5N; stop at 2N; exit on 10-day (S1) or 20-day (S2) channel; max 4 units per market, 6 per correlated group, 10 directional, 12 total — [Original Turtle Rules](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/01/turtle-rules.pdf); [Papers With Backtest summary](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/fighting-the-scams-frauds-and-charlatans-turtle-trading-rules).
- The Turtle rules include a drawdown throttle: reduce position size after account drawdowns (roughly 20% size cut per 10% drawdown) — [Original Turtle Rules](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/01/turtle-rules.pdf).
- Tudor's publicly described rules are codable only in part: 200-day MA filter and 1% risk/5:1 reward-risk are rules, but the entry selection, sizing changes, and macro thesis are discretionary — [Shell Capital](https://shell-capital.com/paul-tudor-jones-200-day-moving-average-rule); [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3738365/as-the-market-crashed-in-1987-paul-tudor-jones-made-100-million-in-a-single-day-the-most-important-rule-of-trading-is-to-play-great-defense-not-great-offense).
- Renaissance's own description — many small predictions, each barely better than chance, diversified by the law of large numbers — implies infrastructure, data, and execution scale, not a single codable signal — [Renaissance Senate statement](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/imo/media/doc/STMT%20-%20Renaissance%20(July%2022%202014)1.pdf).
- Medallion's edge is explicitly capacity-constrained: it stopped accepting outside money in 1993 and distributes profits to keep fund size stable — [Quantt](https://quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history).
- Kullamaggie's setups are describable (breakout, episodic pivot, parabolic short) but require discretionary pattern recognition, news/earnings context, and expensive short locates: "I have access to very good (and sometimes very expensive) locates through my brokers where most other retail brokers do not" — [Qullamaggie FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq).
- Minervini's SEPA is a checklist (trend template, fundamentals, pivot, stop, position size) but the fundamental and "leadership" judgments are discretionary — [Minervini Private Access](http://minerviniprivateaccess.com/about-mark); [TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/mark-minervinis-risk-management).
- Warrior Trading's method depends on live small-cap news, Level 2, short locates, and fast execution; the FTC found the "scalable" claim deceptive for most customers — [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises).

### Inferences
- **Codable today with free/cheap data:** daily Donchian/MA trend on ETFs, futures, FX, crypto; ATR/volatility-target position sizing; cross-sectional momentum; simple breakout scans; defined-risk vertical spreads. These can be backtested and paper-traded by an individual.
- **Codable only with expensive infrastructure:** Medallion-style stat arb (tick data, colocation, financing, thousands of names), convertible arbitrage (inventory, borrow, terms data), merger arb (legal analysis, financing), index rebalancing, options market-making.
- **Not codable at all:** Druckenmiller-style concentrated macro "go for the jugular", Kovner's discretionary geopolitical read, PTJ's intraday feel for crowded positioning. Their *risk rules* can be copied; their *information advantage* cannot.
- A retail trader can copy the **sizing and exit logic** of the Turtles and the **risk budget** of PTJ/Kovner, but not their opportunity set.

### Gaps
- No public source gives Renaissance's actual signals, holding periods, or leverage; any "Medallion strategy" sold publicly should be treated as fiction.
- The precise discretionary inputs in PTJ's, Kovner's, and Druckenmiller's decisions are not documented in a reproducible form.

## 3. What the Turtles' rules and later replications show after costs; what Larry Williams' contest result looked like in subsequent years

### Takeaway
The Turtle program's 80% is a gross, program-level, four-year claim from a favorable commodity-trend regime, with no audit and wide dispersion among individual Turtles. Later tests of turtle-style rules show much weaker results, and the published rules have been widely known since the 1990s/2003. Larry Williams' contest year was followed by two client funds that each lost more than half their capital and closed within about two years — the clearest case that a contest record is not repeatable performance.

### Cited findings
- The Turtles were trained in a complete mechanical system and reportedly earned ~80% average annual compound over four years, ~$175M over five years — [Original Turtle Rules](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/01/turtle-rules.pdf); [Investopedia](https://investopedia.com/articles/trading/08/turtle-trading.asp).
- The program is described as 14 Turtles (other accounts say 21–23 trainees), selected from roughly 1,000 applicants; individual outcomes varied widely — [The 7 Circles](https://the7circles.uk/the-turtle-traders); [Investopedia](https://investopedia.com/articles/trading/08/turtle-trading.asp).
- Curtis Faith, the best-known Turtle, reportedly made ~$30M by age 19, but his later trading firm failed; the $30M figure is not independently audited — [HowToTrade PDF](https://howtotrade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/The-Turtle-Trading-strategy.pdf); [Quantified Strategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.com/turtle-trading-strategy).
- Faith's *Way of the Turtle* tested trend-following variants on 28 US futures markets from 1996 to 2006; one 80-day time-exit variant reportedly returned ~57% annually in that specific test, but this is a **variant selected after the fact**, not the original Turtle system, and the book itself reports mixed results — [Sobrief summary](https://sobrief.com/books/way-of-the-turtle); [Technical Traders article](http://technical.traders.com/tradersonline/display.asp?art=3548).
- The original Turtle rules were published in full in 2003 by OriginalTurtles.org specifically to counter incomplete and fabricated commercial versions — [Papers With Backtest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/fighting-the-scams-frauds-and-charlatans-turtle-trading-rules).
- Trend-following as an industry delivered roughly 0.4% annualized in the 2010s with a 21.8% maximum drawdown on the SG Trend Index — the "lost decade" for CTAs — [Price Action Lab](https://priceactionlab.com/Blog/2023/02/trend-ctas-momentum); [The Investors Podcast](https://theinvestorspodcast.com/blog/market-trends-for-december-2019).
- AQR's century-long simulation finds trend following profitable since 1880, but assumes only a 0.4 Sharpe net of fees and costs in its conservative case — "less than half of what we have observed historically" — [AQR, *A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing*](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing); [AQR PDF](https://proalphacapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/pdfs/a-century-of-evidence-on-trend-following-investing-aqr-capital-management.pdf).
- Short-term trend following has "ceased to deliver reliable returns" since about 2009, according to a 2026 microstructural study, while long-horizon trend has remained positive — [arXiv 2607.01550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01550).
- Larry Williams' 1987 contest account peaked above $2.1M and ended at $1,147,607; the intra-year drawdown was roughly $1M — [Trading Tournaments](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/larry-williams-trading-career).
- In Q1 1987, while his personal account gained $902,599, his composite client accounts lost $6,122,281, per NFA findings — [RealTraders archive](https://purebytes.com/archives/realtraders/1999/msg02468.html).
- His Larry Williams Financial Strategy Fund lost >50% in about a year (closed Oct 1989); the World Cup Championship Fund also lost >50% and closed May 1990 — [RealTraders archive](https://purebytes.com/archives/realtraders/1999/msg02468.html).
- The NFA fined Williams $13,000 and Robbins Trading $15,000 after appeal over promotional/disclosure issues — [RealTraders archive](https://purebytes.com/archives/realtraders/1999/msg02468.html).

### Inferences
- The Turtle 80% figure cannot be attributed to the rules alone: it includes a historic commodity trend, a tiny capital base, 1980s market structure, and a selected group of trainees. Treat it as an upper-bound anecdote, not an expected return.
- After publication, Donchian breakout edges are likely to be smaller and more prone to whipsaw; the 2010s CTA evidence is consistent with crowding and a low-trend macro regime. This is not proof the rules are dead, only that the historical average is not a forecast.
- Williams' case shows the difference between **percent return on a tiny contest account** and **dollar-weighted client outcome**. The contest account's $10k base allowed extreme leverage; the client funds could not survive the same volatility.

### Gaps
- No audited Turtle composite return series exists; the 80% is not verifiable.
- No rigorous, cost-inclusive replication of the *original* Turtle rules over 1990–2020 was found in the sources reviewed; the 57% figure is a variant test and should not be generalized.
- Williams' exact year-by-year personal returns after 1987 are not publicly documented beyond the fund closures.

## 4. Where these methods have decayed after publication or been crowded out — evidence

### Takeaway
The clearest decay evidence is in trend following after 2009 (short-term trend flat/negative; SG Trend ~0.4%/yr in the 2010s), in published equity factors (average post-publication decay ~65%), and in the commercial failure of copied "wizard" methods (Larry Williams' funds, Turtle-style products, Warrior Trading customers). Medallion is the counter-example: it stayed ahead by continuously rebuilding models, not by publishing a static rule.

### Cited findings
- The SG Trend Index returned about 0.4% annualized in the 2010–2019 decade with a 21.8% maximum drawdown — [Price Action Lab](https://priceactionlab.com/Blog/2023/02/trend-ctas-momentum).
- AQR's own trend factor has run at a Sharpe of ~0.34 since its 2012 paper, below every decade in its century of data — [Medium summary of AQR/CFM/Man AHL tests](https://medium.com/@navnoorbawa/how-cfm-aqr-and-man-ahl-tested-trend-following-aqrs-own-century-says-it-broke-6c91c64c1e89).
- Short-term trend following has not delivered reliable returns since ~2009; the paper attributes this to market microstructure changes, while long-term trend persists — [arXiv 2607.01550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01550).
- Across published predictors, long-short returns formed following the original papers decay by ~65% post-publication; mispricing and agnostic predictors decay by ~50% — [arXiv 2212.10317](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10317v1).
- CTA indices suffer survivorship and hindsight bias: studies using the SocGen CTA Index show that the live universe overstates returns because failed managers drop out — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/survivorship-biases/).
- Medallion's response to its 1989 model failure was to stop trading for ~6 months and rebuild, not to keep running the same rules — [Quantt](https://quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history).
- The Turtles' rules were published in full in 2003; the publication itself is evidence that the edge was no longer proprietary — [Papers With Backtest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/fighting-the-scams-frauds-and-charlatans-turtle-trading-rules).
- Warrior Trading's "scalable" small-cap day-trading method was found deceptive for most customers; the FTC said the vast majority of customer accounts lost money — [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises).
- Qullamaggie's momentum style had a massive 2021 (a "market bubble", by his own description) and then a reported ~$60M giveback into 2022 — [Qullamaggie FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq); [Complete Traders Edge](https://completetradersedge.com/kristjan-kullamagi-qullamaggie).

### Inferences
- Decay is **regime- and horizon-specific**, not uniform: long-horizon trend still has a century of evidence, but its Sharpe is modest and its drawdowns are long; short-horizon breakout/momentum is more crowded and more sensitive to microstructure.
- Publication is a useful decay marker. Once a rule is in a book, a contest, or a course, the marginal edge is likely smaller; this is consistent with the factor-decay literature, though the 65% figure is for equity factors, not Turtle rules specifically.
- The "wizard" methods that survived did so through **adaptation and risk control**, not by freezing a 1980s signal. A static copy of a published rule is the weakest version of the method.

### Gaps
- There is no clean natural experiment isolating the effect of Turtle-rule publication on returns; the evidence is indirect (CTA performance, practitioner accounts).
- The 65% post-publication decay estimate comes from equity anomaly papers and may not transfer to trend following or discretionary macro.
- Current (2023–2026) out-of-sample performance of these specific methods was not tested here; no claim should be made that they work today.

## 5. Risk/sizing rules that kept these traders alive, and the drawdowns they tolerated

### Takeaway
The common survival rule is **small risk per bet relative to equity, sized off volatility, with a hard cap on correlated exposure**. The Turtles used 1% of equity per N-unit and a 2N stop; PTJ and Kovner describe ~1% risk per trade and 5:1 reward/risk; Kullamaggie uses 0.3–0.5% and caps positions at 5–25%; Minervini uses 0.5–1.25% with 5–7% stops. Tolerated drawdowns were still large: ~30% for Medallion, 50% for Kullamaggie, >50% for Williams' clients. The "no losing year" records were achieved with much lower per-trade risk and heavy diversification, not with immunity to drawdowns.

### Cited findings
- Turtle sizing: 1 unit = 1% of account equity divided by N (the 20-day EMA of true range); a 2N adverse move = ~2% of equity; max 4 units per market, 6 per correlated group, 10 directional, 12 total — [Original Turtle Rules](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/01/turtle-rules.pdf).
- Turtle drawdown throttle: cut position size after account drawdowns (roughly 20% per 10% drawdown) — [Original Turtle Rules](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/01/turtle-rules.pdf).
- PTJ: ~1% risk per trade, 5:1 reward/risk, "play great defense", never average losers — [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3738365/as-the-market-crashed-in-1987-paul-tudor-jones-made-100-million-in-a-single-day-the-most-important-rule-of-trading-is-to-play-great-defense-not-great-offense); [Gold Legends](https://goldlegendstalesofburiedtreasure.blogspot.com/2026/10/paul-tudor-jones-trading-strategy.html).
- Kovner: 5:1 reward/risk and strict risk control; Caxton's only losing year in 28 was −2.5% in 1994, after which he cut capital and broadened the strategy set — [NYT](https://dealbook.nytimes.com/2011/09/13/bruce-kovner-founder-of-caxton-to-step-down/); [Share Maestro](https://sharemaestro.com/blog/bruce-kovner-caxton-global-macro-risk-control-profile).
- Kullamaggie: 0.3–0.5% risk most of the time, rarely >1%; positions 5–25% (mostly 10–15%); 25% win rate in 2019; biggest drawdown 50% in 2014; tries to contain drawdowns at 15–20% — [Qullamaggie FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq).
- Minervini: 5–7% stops and 0.5–1.25% account risk per trade; 2:1 or better reward/risk — [TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/mark-minervinis-risk-management); [Minervini Private Access](http://minerviniprivateaccess.com/about-mark).
- Thorp used Kelly-criterion reasoning but practical fractional-Kelly sizing; PNP had only 3 losing months out of 230, which implies very small per-bet risk relative to equity — [Chat With Traders](https://chatwithtraders.com/video/edward-thorp-math-genius-who-beat-the-dealer-and-the-market); [LA Times](https://latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-08-05-fi-8302-story.html).
- Medallion's 1989 drawdown was ~30% peak-to-trough, and the fund survived by stopping and rebuilding; its later risk model relied on many small, diversified bets — [Quantt](https://quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history); [Renaissance Senate statement](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/imo/media/doc/STMT%20-%20Renaissance%20(July%2022%202014)1.pdf).
- Druckenmiller ran concentrated positions and did not use a fixed stop; he described an early $20–25M loss as formative — [Morgan Stanley "Hard Lessons"](https://morganstanley.com/insights/videos/hard-lessons/duquesne-stan-druckenmiller-iliana-bouzali).
- Larry Williams' client funds lost >50% in about a year, showing the absence of a drawdown cap in the copied high-leverage method — [RealTraders archive](https://purebytes.com/archives/realtraders/1999/msg02468.html).

### Inferences
- The risk rules are the **most transferable** part of the whole sample. A 0.3–1% risk per trade with a hard stop and a correlated-exposure cap is codable, testable, and scale-free.
- The returns are not scale-free. A 1% risk budget on $147 is $1.47; on $10M it is $100,000. The same percentage produces very different commission/spread drag.
- Drawdown tolerance is a **survival constraint**, not a preference. Medallion could stop for six months; a retail account with $147 cannot earn its way back if it risks 10% per trade.
- "No losing year" is not the same as "small drawdown": a fund can be up 2% for the year after a 25% intra-year drawdown. Always ask for peak-to-trough, not calendar-year.

### Gaps
- Kovner's exact per-trade risk percentage is not consistently sourced; the 1% figure is widely repeated but should be treated as approximate.
- PTJ's and Druckenmiller's peak-to-trough drawdowns are not public.
- No source gives Medallion's realized leverage or risk budget per position.

## 6. What a $147 account can realistically copy from each, and what is impossible

### Takeaway
A $147 account (≈5,000 THB, the TradeLab paper starting capital) can copy the **process** — 1% risk, ATR/volatility sizing, daily-bar breakout/trend rules, journaling, and defined-risk structures — on fractional shares or crypto. It cannot copy the **returns** or the **opportunity set**: Medallion's stat arb, macro scale, hard-to-borrow shorts, US pattern-day-trading, and multi-leg arbitrage are all closed off by capital, access, or regulation. At $147, the binding constraint is not the strategy; it is fixed costs (commissions, spreads, FX conversion) relative to a $1.47 risk unit.

### Cited findings
- US pattern-day-trader rule: a margin account needs $25,000 equity to make 4+ day trades in 5 business days; below that, day-trading US stocks is effectively prohibited — [FINRA Rule 4210](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4210).
- Micro E-mini S&P 500 futures carry initial margin in the ~$1,000+ range, far above a $147 account; micro forex lots are 1,000 units, so a 20-pip stop risks about $2 (≈1.4% of $147), already above the 1% target — [CME Micro E-mini](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/equities/sp/micro-e-mini-sandp-500.html); [Investopedia forex lots](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/s/standard-lot.asp).
- Fractional shares and fractional crypto let a $147 account hold positions smaller than one share/coin; this is the only practical way to implement 1% risk across multiple instruments — [Investopedia fractional shares](https://www.investopedia.com/fractional-shares-5113003).
- Interactive Brokers charges ~$0.005/share with a $1 minimum per order (varies by tier); a $15 position with a $1 round-trip commission loses ~6.7% before the trade moves — [IBKR commissions](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-stocks.php).
- Kullamaggie's 0.3–0.5% risk and 5–25% positions imply, at $147, $0.44–$0.74 risk and $7–$37 positions — sizes at which spreads and commissions dominate — [Qullamaggie FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq).
- The Turtle 1%-of-equity unit at $147 is $1.47; with N often $1–$3 for a commodity ETF, the minimum position can still be several dollars, and the 2N stop risks ~$2.94 — feasible only with fractional instruments — [Original Turtle Rules](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/01/turtle-rules.pdf).
- Medallion is closed to outside investors and was bought out by 2005; there is no retail access — [Quantt](https://quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history).
- Kullamaggie explicitly says he has access to expensive short locates that most retail brokers do not — [Qullamaggie FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq).
- Warrior Trading's small-cap method requires live news, Level 2, and fast execution; the FTC found most customers lost money even after paying for the training — [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises).
- US Investing Championship entries are verified by brokerage statements, but the contest is a one-year, high-concentration sprint, not a scalable account strategy — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/stock-trader-wins-us-investing-championship-a-second-time--breaks-record-301466652.html).

### What $147 can actually test (concrete)
- **Trend/breakout on fractional ETFs or crypto:** daily 20/55-day Donchian, 1% risk ($1.47), 2N stop; 20–30 trades to see whether the process is executable. Use commission-free or near-zero-cost venues; crypto spreads/funding are the cost.
- **Volatility sizing:** compute ATR(20) and set position = risk$ / (2×ATR). This is the single most transferable Turtle lesson.
- **Defined-risk options:** one cheap vertical spread or a long option with max loss ≤ $7–$15 (5–10% of account) is possible, but only as a test; commissions and spreads can exceed the edge.
- **Journal and out-of-sample:** 100+ trades, pre-registered rules, no changes mid-test; compare to a random-entry/same-sizing benchmark.
- **Do not expect income.** At 20%/yr, $147 becomes ~$176 in a year; at 100%/yr, ~$294. The realistic value is learning whether the rule set survives costs and whether the trader can follow it.

### Inferences
- The $147 account is a **laboratory, not a business**. Its correct use is to validate execution, sizing, and psychology on fractional instruments, then scale only after a documented, cost-inclusive sample.
- The most faithful copy is **Turtle risk logic + a simple trend/breakout signal on liquid fractional instruments**; the least faithful and most dangerous is **small-cap momentum day trading**, which requires capital, locates, speed, and is the exact area where regulators found most customers lost money.
- Any claim that a $147 account can replicate 30–268% CAGR is not supported by the documented evidence; those returns came from institutional scale, extreme leverage in favorable regimes, or contest selection.
- **Reality check if the goal is 3× per month (≈200% monthly, an earlier TradeLab target):** none of the documented records shows this as a repeatable rate. Medallion's reported long-run average is 66% gross/39% net **per year**. Kullamaggie's best documented year, 2021, was +870% (≈20%/month compounded) and was followed by a reported ~$60M giveback. Williams' contest year was +11,376% (≈47%/month compounded) on a $10k leveraged account, followed by two client funds losing >50%. A 3× monthly target is therefore a lottery-tail outcome, not a durable method supported by this sample.

### Gaps
- No study was found that tests the original Turtle rules on a $147 fractional account, net of realistic retail commissions and spreads; this is an open out-of-sample question.
- Current broker minimums, fractional-share availability, and crypto minimum notionals vary by country/venue and were not verified for the user's jurisdiction.
- The user's actual broker, instruments, and cost schedule are unknown; the cost estimates above are illustrative, not a quote.
